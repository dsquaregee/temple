# Achyutaraya Temple (Tiruvengalanatha), Hampi (achyutaraya-hampi)

- Deity: Vishnu as Tiruvengalanatha (Venkateshwara, Lord of Tirumala/Venkata). Ruined, not in active worship; UNESCO Hampi group; ASI.
- Coordinates: TripAdvisor geodata 15.3327, 76.47012 (~1 km E of Virupaksha). Used 15.3327, 76.4701. (Wikipedia's page shows the Hampi town point 15.3344, 76.4622, not the temple.)
- History: 1534 CE, reign of Achyuta Deva Raya (r. 1529–42); built by a high officer of his court and known by the king's name rather than the deity's. One of the last grand temple projects in the capital before 1565.
- Plan: two concentric enclosures (prakaras), gopurams on the north; a wide, long colonnaded street (Sule Bazaar / "courtesans' street") runs north from the temple toward the river; temple lies in a secluded valley between Gandhamadana and Matanga hills; kalyana mandapa in the outer court.
- Access: from Kodandarama temple along riverside path, or steps near the monolithic Nandi at the east end of Hampi Bazaar (over Matanga hill shoulder).
- Unverified: name of the officer (Hiriya Tirumalaraja / Salakaraju Tirumala) — not used; sanctum facing.
