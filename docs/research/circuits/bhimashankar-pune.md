# Bhimashankar Temple, Bhimashankar (bhimashankar-pune)

- Deity: Shiva as Bhimashankar; one of the twelve Jyotirlingas (Dvadasha Jyotirlinga Stotram: "Dakinyam Bhimashankaram"). A tradition in Assam also claims the "Dakini" Bhimashankar; the Sahyadri shrine is the widely revered one (mentioned only briefly).
- Location: Bhimashankar, Khed taluka, Pune district, Maharashtra; crest of the Sahyadri (Western Ghats), c. 110 km from Pune by road; inside Bhimashankar Wildlife Sanctuary (Indian giant squirrel, "shekru", Maharashtra's state animal).
- Coordinates: 19.0720, 73.5361 (Wikipedia infobox 19°04′19″N 73°32′10″E).
- Source of the Bhima river (tributary of the Krishna); "Gupt Bhimashankar" spring trail nearby.
- History: shrine and river mentioned in literature from c. 13th century; black-stone temple; sabhamandap and shikhara renewed/improved in the 18th century by Nana Phadnavis (Peshwa minister). A large bell associated with Chimaji Appa (brought after the Vasai campaign, 1739).
- Style: Nagara, with Hemadpanthi (Deccan) elements; dark basalt; carved figures on walls.
- Legend: demon Bhima, son of Kumbhakarna, terrorised gods and devotees; Shiva destroyed him here and remained as Bhimashankar; sweat of Shiva after the battle said to have formed the Bhima river.
- Festivals: Maha Shivaratri (fair), Shravan Mondays, Kartik (Tripuri) Purnima — confirm dates locally. Monsoon brings mist and many trekkers.
- Getting there: road from Pune via Rajgurunagar (Khed) and Manchar; nearest rail/airport Pune; trekking routes up the ghats from the Karjat side.
