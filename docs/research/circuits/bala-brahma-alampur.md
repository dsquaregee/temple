# Bala Brahma Temple, Alampur (bala-brahma-alampur)

- Coordinates used: 15.8775, 78.1357 (estimated; see note).
- Group: nine Early (Badami) Chalukya Shiva temples at Alampur, Jogulamba Gadwal dist., Telangana, left bank of the Tungabhadra near the Krishna confluence; ASI-protected; named Bala, Kumara, Arka, Vira, Vishwa, Garuda, Swarga, Padma, Taraka Brahma(eshwara). Tradition: Brahma did penance here and worshipped Shiva; the nine names are the forms in which he is remembered. Standing temples datable from mid-7th c. (ASI); earliest inscription (Vikramaditya I, 654–681) records a linga installed in the Arka Brahma temple. Most sikharas are northern curvilinear (rekha-nagara, 3–4 bhumis, karna-amalakas). Damage in post-14th-c. raids; Islamic dargah compound abuts the NW of the group.
- Coordinates: WebFetch/curl to Commons/OSM/Wikidata blocked in this environment; no per-temple published coordinate found. Point ESTIMATED from Commons camera geotags (Svarga 15.877367,78.135536 & 15.877231,78.135361; Garuda 15.877014,78.135583 heading N; Bala via PICRYL mirror 15.87751,78.13579), the Wikipedia group point (15.877806,78.134833) and relative positions in trawell (Garuda south of Bala; Swarga behind Bala; Padma behind Swarga; Taraka behind the museum; Vishwa/Vira/Arka/Kumara row to the north, Kumara opposite museum). Accuracy perhaps ±30–50 m; worth checking against the ASI site plan.

- Deity: Shiva as Bala Brahmeshwara (Bala Brahma); the only Navabrahma shrine in active worship; principal temple of the group. Linga traditionally said to have the form of a bull's hoof-print (several devotional/travel sources).
- Jogulamba link: after the Jogulamba shrine was destroyed in 1390, the goddess's image was kept and worshipped in this temple until her new temple was consecrated in 2005 beside it.
- Date/inscription: dated to the late 7th c. (completion c. 650 per Commons/Wikipedia captions; others attribute to Vinayaditya/Vijayaditya). A Sanskrit pillar inscription of Vijayaditya's reign (696–733) celebrates Vinayaditya's victory over the "lord of Uttarapatha" (north India) and seizure of the insignia of Malwa and Lata rulers (telangana360 inscription survey). Pulakeshin II attribution (trawell) conflicts — omitted.
- Architecture: largest / most striking of the group; Nagara curvilinear tower; mukha-mandapa, maha-mandapa, sanctum with inner circumambulatory passage, plus pillared verandah around for outer pradakshina; perforated stone windows; empty exterior niches. Inner hall: Mahishasuramardini, Saptamatrikas, Indra, Agni; large dancing Shiva on the ceiling; small subsidiary shrines (incl. Nataraja).
- Festivals: Maha Shivaratri major; worshipped together with Jogulamba at Navaratri/Dasara; Jogulamba Brahmotsavam.
- NW side encroached by later Islamic structures (Commons).
