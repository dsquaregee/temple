# Malyavanta Raghunatha Temple, Hampi (malyavanta-raghunatha-hampi)

- Coordinates used: 15.3244, 76.4868 — Tripadvisor geo 15.324322, 76.487045; Justdial 15.324407, 76.4865619; Evendo plus code 8FFQ+R25 (7J7R8FFQ+R25 decodes 15.32451, 76.48761). Three listings agree within ~100 m.
- Location: on Malyavanta hill, east of the Urban Core of Vijayanagara, south of the Vittala temple area, about 7 km by road from Hampi Bazaar; road to the top.
- UNESCO: the Karnataka Tourism VR-GIS portal (hampi360) describes the Raghunatha temple complex on Malyavanta hill as rising above the east end of the Urban Core, i.e. within the ruined city that forms the Group of Monuments at Hampi. UNESCO's summary does not name it among highlighted complexes; no official core-zone list found. Set unesco: true on the basis that the hill lies within the city/urban core of the property — FLAG for lead.
- Deity: Rama as Raghunatha, with Sita, Lakshmana and kneeling Hanuman carved on a massive boulder that forms the sanctum; the boulder rises through the roof and is crowned by a built votive tower (shikhara).
- Architecture: five-storeyed gopura on the south, three-storeyed on the east; enclosure with a Devi shrine and small Shiva shrine; nearby a cleft in the rock with rows of carved Shiva lingas and Nandis.
- Inscription: Saka 1481 = 1559 CE, reign of Sadasiva Raya: grant of land to "Malyavanta-Raghunathadeva" for a water trough (South Indian Inscriptions vol. 9); village Chhatradahalli was a manya of the god.
- Legend: Malyavanta identified with Mount Prasravana of the Ramayana (Kishkindha Kanda), where Rama and Lakshmana waited out the monsoon after Sugriva's coronation, before the search for Sita/ war against Ravana.
- Living temple; bhajans; sunset viewpoint.
