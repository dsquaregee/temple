# Vishwa Brahma Temple, Alampur (vishwa-brahma-alampur)

- Also Vishva/Visva Brahma. One of the nine Navabrahma temples, Alampur (group context in kumara-brahma-alampur.md). ASI-protected; not in worship.
- Coordinates used: 15.8790, 78.1353 (ESTIMATED).
- Coordinates: no per-shrine geotag (Wikipedia/Wikidata/Commons/OSM) was retrievable — Commons/OSM/Wikidata APIs are blocked from this environment and search snippets give only the group point. Position ESTIMATED from the documented relative layout (trawell: Kumara is south of Arka, opposite the site museum; Arka south of Vira; Vishwa in the northern corner beside Vira, opposite the museum) — the four stand in a short N–S row, placed ~30 m apart north of Bala Brahma, east of the site museum, consistent with the sibling estimates (Bala 15.8775,78.1357; Swarga 15.8778,78.1348; Padma 15.8779,78.1344; Taraka 15.8786,78.1347 behind the museum; Garuda 15.8771,78.1356 — from Commons camera geotags + trawell) and with Kumara 'opposite the museum'. Accuracy perhaps ±30–60 m. Lead should verify against a map before publish.
- Location: northern corner of the precinct, opposite the museum, beside Vira Brahma (trawell).
- Plan: like Swarga Brahma but without the porch (hindupad); compared with the Galaganatha temple at Pattadakal (trawell). Large maha-mandapa (trawell says 18 pillars — figure not used) with floral-carved pillars; sandhara sanctum; curvilinear tower with amalaka, well sculpted (Deccan Herald). Best preserved of the group after Bala Brahma (trawell).
- Sculpture: sanctum doorway — Gajalakshmi; Ganga and Yamuna with their mounts (crocodile, tortoise). Rear of the sanctum: Bhagiratha's penance to bring Ganga to earth. Exterior niches with kirtimukha; images of Vishnu, Nataraja, Ganesha, Surya, Chandra, gandharvas, yakshis. Singled out (esamskriti/ASI-derived): Dakshinamurti seated in utkutikasana, Trivikrama, and the boyish Kartikeya with peacock standard. Many faces damaged by axe (hindupad).
- Date: 7th–8th c. (sources vary; some place it in the later phase); century 8.
