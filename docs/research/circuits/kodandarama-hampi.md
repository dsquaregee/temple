# Kodandarama Temple, Hampi (kodandarama-hampi)

- Coordinates used: 15.3360, 76.4684 — Justdial listing geo 15.3359792, 76.4684355; matches Wanderlog plus code 8FP9+99V (decodes to 15.33599, 76.46842). Two listings agree (likely same map source); not from Wikipedia/OSM.
- Setting: on the south bank of the Tungabhadra at Chakratirtha, the bathing spot considered the holiest on the river at Hampi; on the riverside footpath from Hampi Bazaar (east end / below Matanga hill) to the Vittala temple. The Yantrodharaka Anjaneya shrine is on the rocks nearby.
- Deities: Rama (Kodanda — holding the bow), Sita and Lakshmana carved in deep relief on a huge boulder, which forms the sanctum's back wall; a small monkey figure locally said to be Sugriva rather than Hanuman.
- Legend (local tradition): place where Sugriva was crowned king of Kishkindha after Vali's death (one listing says Lakshmana performed the crowning — conflict; content says "Rama had Sugriva crowned" phrased as tradition); tradition that Sugriva carved the images.
- Living temple: worship continues; (festivals: Rama Navami customary — not specifically confirmed, wording "confirm dates locally").
- Date: shrine structures of Vijayanagara period (not firmly dated in sources found) — century set at 16 as approximate.
- Status: inside the Hampi UNESCO property (riverside sacred centre).
