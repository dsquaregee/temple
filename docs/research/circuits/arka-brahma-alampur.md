# Arka Brahma Temple, Alampur (arka-brahma-alampur)

- Group: one of the nine Navabrahma temples, Alampur (see kumara-brahma-alampur.md for group context). ASI-protected; not in worship.
- Coordinates used: 15.8784, 78.1354 (ESTIMATED).
- Coordinates: no per-shrine geotag (Wikipedia/Wikidata/Commons/OSM) was retrievable — Commons/OSM/Wikidata APIs are blocked from this environment and search snippets give only the group point. Position ESTIMATED from the documented relative layout (trawell: Kumara is south of Arka, opposite the site museum; Arka south of Vira; Vishwa in the northern corner beside Vira, opposite the museum) — the four stand in a short N–S row, placed ~30 m apart north of Bala Brahma, east of the site museum, consistent with the sibling estimates (Bala 15.8775,78.1357; Swarga 15.8778,78.1348; Padma 15.8779,78.1344; Taraka 15.8786,78.1347 behind the museum; Garuda 15.8771,78.1356 — from Commons camera geotags + trawell) and with Kumara 'opposite the museum'. Accuracy perhaps ±30–60 m. Lead should verify against a map before publish.
- Location: between Kumara Brahma (to its south) and Vira Brahma (to its north), opposite the museum.
- Inscription: ASI Hyderabad Circle — earliest known inscription at the group is of the reign of Vikramaditya I (654–681 CE), referring to the installation of a linga in the Mahadevayatana (shrine of Mahadeva) of the Arka-Brahma temple; it is a pillar inscription (telangana360). Blog sources add that it names queen Mahadevi as installer and records a land grant (50 nivartanas at Vaddamanu to a Brahmana) — NOT used (blog, unverified; "Mahadevi" may be a misreading of Mahadevayatana).
- Condition: the most ruined of the nine — roof gone, sculpture largely destroyed; remnants of Ganga and Yamuna on the sanctum doorway; plan of maha-mandapa, sanctum and verandah (trawell itinerary/esamskriti). Damage attributed broadly to the wars from the 14th c. on (Wikipedia); trawell names a Bahmani sultan — not used.
- Name: arka = the sun (also the arka plant); no shrine-specific legend found.
