# Kumara Brahma Temple, Alampur (kumara-brahma-alampur)

- Group: one of the nine Navabrahma temples (Bala, Taraka, Swarga, Padma, Garuda, Kumara, Arka, Vira, Vishwa Brahma), Alampur, Jogulamba Gadwal dist., Telangana, left bank of the Tungabhadra near its confluence with the Krishna. All nine are Shiva shrines (lingas) though named "Brahma"; tradition: Brahma did penance here and consecrated nine lingas. ASI-protected. Not a living temple (only Bala Brahma is in worship).
- Coordinates used: 15.8781, 78.1354 (ESTIMATED; see below).
- Coordinates: no per-shrine geotag (Wikipedia/Wikidata/Commons/OSM) was retrievable — Commons/OSM/Wikidata APIs are blocked from this environment and search snippets give only the group point. Position ESTIMATED from the documented relative layout (trawell: Kumara is south of Arka, opposite the site museum; Arka south of Vira; Vishwa in the northern corner beside Vira, opposite the museum) — the four stand in a short N–S row, placed ~30 m apart north of Bala Brahma, east of the site museum, consistent with the sibling estimates (Bala 15.8775,78.1357; Swarga 15.8778,78.1348; Padma 15.8779,78.1344; Taraka 15.8786,78.1347 behind the museum; Garuda 15.8771,78.1356 — from Commons camera geotags + trawell) and with Kumara 'opposite the museum'. Accuracy perhaps ±30–60 m. Lead should verify against a map before publish.
- Location: southern side of the Arka Brahma temple, directly opposite the ASI site museum (trawell).
- Plan: Rekha-nagara (curvilinear northern) type; on a high platform; mukha-mandapa (pillared porch, mukha-chatushki — ASI notes only Svarga and Kumara Brahma have this), maha-mandapa, sanctum with enclosed circumambulatory path (sandhara). ASI: the shukanasa over the antarala is integral with the tower in the group, EXCEPT Kumara Brahma, which has a flat roof. (telangana360 claims a tri-tala shikhara — conflicting, not used.)
- Sculpture: maha-mandapa pillars carved with Gajalakshmi, Uma-Maheshwara, Kubera, elephants, swans, figures riding mythical beasts; sanctum doorway with Vishnu and floral toranas (trawell).
- Date: group mid-7th c. (ASI); trawell suggests Vikramaditya I's time (655–681) — "thought to"; several writers call it among the earliest of the group (unconfirmed, not stated as fact).
- Name: kumara = "youth"; no specific legend found beyond the group's Brahma-penance tradition.
- Festivals: precinct observances — Maha Shivaratri (Bala Brahmeshwara), Navaratri/Dasara (Jogulamba).
