# Vira Brahma Temple, Alampur (vira-brahma-alampur)

- Also spelt Veera Brahma. One of the nine Navabrahma temples, Alampur (group context in kumara-brahma-alampur.md). ASI-protected; not in worship.
- Coordinates used: 15.8787, 78.1354 (ESTIMATED).
- Coordinates: no per-shrine geotag (Wikipedia/Wikidata/Commons/OSM) was retrievable — Commons/OSM/Wikidata APIs are blocked from this environment and search snippets give only the group point. Position ESTIMATED from the documented relative layout (trawell: Kumara is south of Arka, opposite the site museum; Arka south of Vira; Vishwa in the northern corner beside Vira, opposite the museum) — the four stand in a short N–S row, placed ~30 m apart north of Bala Brahma, east of the site museum, consistent with the sibling estimates (Bala 15.8775,78.1357; Swarga 15.8778,78.1348; Padma 15.8779,78.1344; Taraka 15.8786,78.1347 behind the museum; Garuda 15.8771,78.1356 — from Commons camera geotags + trawell) and with Kumara 'opposite the museum'. Accuracy perhaps ±30–60 m. Lead should verify against a map before publish.
- Location: north of Arka Brahma, south of (beside) Vishwa Brahma, opposite the museum (trawell).
- Features: badly damaged; door guardians (dvarapalas) on either side of the sanctum door, one badly worn; a Nataraja (dancing Shiva) carved on the vimana/tower (temple-blog description surfaced via search). Common group plan: east-facing sanctum with enclosed circumambulatory, mandapa, Rekha-nagara curvilinear tower, decorative engaged pilasters (kudya-stambhas).
- Date: group 7th–8th c.; some writers place the northern shrines later (8th c., with Rashtrakuta-period work) — no shrine-specific date; century set 8 as a cautious "7th–8th c." placement.
- Name: vira = "hero".
- Unconfirmed: pillar/hall details specific to Vira Brahma.
