# Veerattaneswarar Temple, Thiruvathigai (veerattaneswarar-thiruvathigai)

Circuit: ashta-veerattanam. Tradition: Shaivism. Veerattanam deed: Tripura samhara (Tripurantaka, destroyer of the three cities).

- Deity: Shiva as Veerattaneswarar (Veerattanathar), lingam; consort Thiripurasundari; separate Periyanayaki shrine with a colourful vimana in which a twelve-armed Tripurantaka stands out (shivatemples.com).
- Location: Thiruvathigai, ~2 km from Panruti, Cuddalore district, on the north bank of the Kedilam (Gadilam) river.
- Coordinates: Wikipedia infobox 11°46′06″N 79°34′31″E -> 11.7683, 79.5753 (Bharatpedia's 11°28′N 79°20′E is a rough district point, rejected).
- Paadal Petra Sthalam (Nadu Nadu); Appar is credited with 16 pathigams here (shivatemples.com); also sung by Sambandar and Sundarar per tradition lists.
- Appar story: Dharmasenar (later Appar/Thirunavukkarasar), then a Jain monk, afflicted by a severe stomach ailment (soolai), came to his sister Thilagavathiyar who served at this temple; given holy ash, cured; composed his first hymn "Kootrayinavaru vilakkagileer". Hence the temple is where Appar returned to Shaivism. (Wikipedia also says he "attained salvation" here — conflicts with standard tradition of Thirupugalur; omitted.)
- Pallava king Mahendravarman I, converted from Jainism through Appar's influence; believed to have built Gunavareswaram (Gunadharaeswaram) near Thiruvathigai.
- Thilagavathiyar has a shrine in the first precinct; a pillared hall believed built at her behest (Bharatpedia/Wikipedia).
- Architecture: large complex, two gopurams (Wikipedia); five-tiered gopuram to inner sanctum (another listing).
- Not verified: specific inscriptions, festival calendar details (Chithirai festivals generic) — kept generic.
