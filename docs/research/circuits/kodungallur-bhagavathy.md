# Kodungallur Sree Kurumba Bhagavathy Temple (kodungallur-bhagavathy)

- Deity: Bhadrakali, worshipped as Kurumba Bhagavathy / Kurumba Amma, in ugra (fierce) form; sanctum faces north. A Shiva shrine within the complex faces east (travel listing). Described as head of the 64 Bhadrakali kavus of Malabar.
- Location: Kodungallur, Thrissur district, Kerala. Coordinates: Kerala Tourism 10.22686, 76.19616; Wikipedia infobox 10°13'37"N 76°11'54"E (10.2269, 76.1983). Used 10.2269, 76.1962 (Kerala Tourism). Audit's 10.2270, 76.1960 confirmed.
- History: counted among the oldest temples of Kerala; Kodungallur is the ancient port identified with Muziris (traders from Rome, Arabia, China). Origin legends differ: Parasurama installing the goddess; or Chera king Chenkuttuvan installing Kannaki (disputed by historians). Present form medieval (undated). Chera-era association is tradition.
- Festivals: Bharani festival from the Bharani asterism in Kumbham (March-April) to seven days after Bharani asterism in Meenam; Kavu Theendal (oracles with sabres run around the temple; offerings thrown into the inner quadrangle), Bharani songs, oracles' ritual abuse of the goddess (bawdy invective), purification next day, Ashwathy-day circumambulation striking brass sheets. Animal sacrifice (cocks) now banned; arrests reported 2022. Historians note the temple doors were open to Dalit and other communities during Bharani before the Temple Entry Proclamation. Thalappoli in Makaram/Jan-Feb (sources vary).
- Unverified: architectural specifics, exact construction dates, Kavu Theendal-to-Ashwathy sequence.
