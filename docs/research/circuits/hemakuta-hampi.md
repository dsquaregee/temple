# Hemakuta Hill group of temples, Hampi (hemakuta-hampi)

- Coordinates used: 15.3343, 76.4585 — Mappls listing "Hemakuta Hill Temple, Hampi" (eLoc 8FT447; embedded zdata decodes to 15.334318, 76.458526). Distinct from Virupaksha (15.335, 76.4599), which lies just north/north-east below the hill.
- Hill rising south of Virupaksha temple and above Hampi Bazaar; once fortified with stone walls; dozens of small shrines, gateways and pavilions.
- Dating: many shrines 9th–14th c., i.e. before the founding of Vijayanagara (c. 1336); earliest Shiva shrines with stepped pyramidal granite vimanas assigned to ~9th–10th c.; others to the earliest phase of Vijayanagara (14th c.).
- Trikuta (trikutachala) temples on the northern side: three shrines set at right angles round a shared central hall; often mistaken for Jain temples because of the stepped roofs; primarily Shaiva (lingas).
- Mula Virupaksha shrine on the southern part — tradition that it is older than the great Virupaksha temple; front hall with cubical pre-Vijayanagara pillars.
- Nearby on the hill slopes: Sasivekalu and Kadalekalu monolithic Ganeshas (southern side, towards Krishna temple).
- Legends: Shiva's penance on Hemakuta before marrying Pampa (Parvati); Kama (Manmatha) disturbed the penance and was burned by Shiva's third eye; Rati pleaded and Kama was revived without a body; gold rained on the hill when Shiva agreed to marry Pampa, hence "Hema-kuta" (golden peak).
- Status: UNESCO names the Hemakuta complex among highlighted remains; ASI-protected, open hill, sunset viewpoint.
- Tradition: Shaivism. Not in active worship (monuments).
