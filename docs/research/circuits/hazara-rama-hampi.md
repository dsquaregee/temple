# Hazara Rama Temple, Hampi (hazara-rama-hampi)

- Deity: Rama, as Ramachandra. Royal (state/private) temple of the Vijayanagara kings in the royal centre; no active worship, sanctum empty. Part of UNESCO Group of Monuments at Hampi; ASI.
- Coordinates: no published point found via search (Wikipedia/OSM unreachable). Used 15.3192, 76.4683 — map-based estimate of the temple at the crossroads between the royal enclosure, noblemen's quarter and the zenana/Lotus Mahal; consistent with Hampi town point 15.3344, 76.4622 and Uddana Veerabhadra 15.3262, 76.4600. FLAG: should be confirmed against OSM.
- History: inscriptions indicate Devaraya I (r. 1406–22) erected it as a shrine to Rama under the name Ramachandra (VRP; V&A). Many tourism sites say Devaraya II — conflict; VRP preferred. Open porch mandapa possibly a 16th-c. addition.
- Name: "Hazara Rama" = "a thousand Ramas" for the profusion of Ramayana reliefs.
- Architecture: enclosure wall outer face — friezes of processions of elephants, horses with attendants, soldiers, dancing women, as in the Mahanavami festival described by foreign visitors; inner face and temple walls — Ramayana narrative in bands, read clockwise (circumambulation), from Dasharatha's sacrifice for sons (putrakameshti) and Rama's birth through exile, abduction of Sita, war with Ravana. Mandapa with polished black-stone pillars carved with deities, on a raised platform.
- Location: centre of the royal area, ~3 km from Hampi Bazaar.
- Visit: ASI monument; confirm hours/ticket locally.
