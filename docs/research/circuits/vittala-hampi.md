# Vijaya Vittala Temple, Hampi (vittala-hampi)

- Deity: Vishnu as Vittala (Vijaya Vitthala), the form worshipped at Pandharpur. Not in active worship; ASI-protected monument within the UNESCO Group of Monuments at Hampi (1986).
- Coordinates: no infobox reachable; Wikimedia Commons photo geotags at the temple: 15.34233, 76.47573 (stone chariot, Arian Zwegers) and 15.34168, 76.47541 (left gopuram relief, Ingo Mehling). Used 15.3420, 76.4756 (between them; inside the complex).
- History: core of the temple from Devaraya II (1422–46); bulk of present structure under Krishnadevaraya (1509–29). VRP notes original patron unknown, possibly Vira Narasimha (Tuluva) early 16th c. 23 inscriptions 1513–1564 record worship of Vijaya Vitthala; Krishnadevaraya built the hundred-columned hall; two of his queens each added a gopura; a third gopura attributed to Achyutaraya. Krishnadevaraya donated silver and gold vessels.
- Architecture: complex with Amman shrine, kalyana mandapa, natya mandapa, 100-pillared sabha mandapa; maha mandapa of 56 pillars (~3.6 m), its slender clustered pillars ring musically when tapped (now off-limits by ASI); stone chariot (Garuda shrine) on the axis facing the mandapa — one of three famous stone chariots (with Konark and Mahabalipuram), on the ₹50 note; wheels once turned, now fixed.
- Legend: Vittala of Pandharpur is said to have found the temple too grand and returned to his humble home, leaving it unconsecrated (folk tradition; inscriptions show worship did occur).
- Location: NE of Hampi near the Tungabhadra, opposite Anegondi, ~2.5 km from Hampi Bazaar. Electric shuttle from parking.
- Visit: ASI ticketed.
