# Velvidai Nathar (Velladai Nathar) Temple, Tirukkurukavur (velvidainathar-tirukkurukavur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name:
Tirukkurukavur Velladai, திருக்குருகாவூர் வெள்ளடை). District: Mayiladuthurai (Sirkazhi
taluk; older sources / postal address still say Nagapattinam district; village is also
written Thirukkarukavur / Vadakal in some listings). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Velvidai Nathar / Velladai Nathar / Velladai Eswarar (Sveta Rishabheswarar,
  "Lord of the White Bull"); consort Kaviyankanni (also Neelotpala Visalakshi), separate
  south-facing shrine. Sthala vriksha: bilva. Theertham: Paal Kinaru (milk well) plus temple
  tank. (Do not confuse with Tirukkarukavur / Mullaivananathar near Kumbakonam — a different
  temple.)
- Tevaram: sung by Sambandar and Sundarar. Counted as the 13th Tevaram Shiva temple on the
  northern bank of the Kaveri.
- Coordinates: **approximate ~11.232, 79.789** — inferred from sources placing the temple ~6 km
  from Sirkazhi on the Sirkazhi–Tirumullaivasal road, 1 km off the road at Vadakal. No
  sourced lat/lng found; refine against a mapping source.
- Legends: (a) Vishnu took the form of a white bull (vellai edabam) and worshipped Shiva —
  origin of "Velvidai/Velladai", Rishabhapuram, Vishnupuram. (b) Shiva as an old man fed
  Sambandar and his party curd rice and shelter; house vanished when Sambandar woke —
  place called Dadyonnapuram ("curd rice town"). (c) Shiva brought the Ganga into the
  well for Sambandar (who wished to bathe at Kashi to atone for harm from debates with rival
  faiths) — the Paal Kinaru, said to turn milky white in the Tamil month of Thai. Wikipedia
  instead attributes the Jain-debate expiation to Sundarar; sources conflict — prose follows
  tntemplesproject (Sambandar) and states Sundarar sang a pathigam.
- **Seed-hint correction:** the brief's "famine/rice legend — Lord wears a white garment
  (velladai)" was NOT found. "Velladai/Velvidai" is explained in sources by the white bull
  (Vishnu worshipping as Rishabha); the rice element is the curd-rice meal for Sambandar.
  The famine echo is only the devotee belief (tntemplesproject) that worship here prevents
  food shortage, used lightly in the festivals section. The "white garment" reading was not used.
- Other traditions found but not used in prose: Agni as a pigeon (Sibi tale) worshipped
  here and made a tank, giving "Kuruku/kuruvi"-based village name; Kubera/gems
  ("Ratnagireswarar"); a "child in the womb" tale (likely mis-attributed from Tirukkarukavur).
- Architecture: Early Chola temple (probably 7th c.), rebuilt in stone in the late 10th century
  under Uttama Chola; inscriptions of Rajendra Chola, Kulottunga I, Vikrama Chola; shrines
  to Ganesha, Durga, Murugan with Valli and Deivanai; last kumbhabhishekam 18 Jun 1986. No
  detailed gopuram/vimana/prakaram description found, so none given.
- Festivals: Thai Amavasya (Jan-Feb) theerthavari — Paal Kinaru opened/white; Sambandar
  idol brought from Sirkazhi Brahmapureeswarar temple (Dharisanam); Chithirai full moon
  (Apr-May) curd-rice feeding; Thai Poosam, Skanda Sashti, Maha Shivaratri, Pradosham.
  Dates differ across sources (Thai Amavasya vs Pournami) — stated as Amavasya.
- Access: ~6 km from Sirkazhi (nearest railway), ~25 km from Mayiladuthurai and Chidambaram;
  Sirkazhi–Tirumullaivasal bus, alight at Vadakal, ~1 km walk; nearest airport listed:
  Tiruchirappalli.
- **Not verified:** exact coordinate; sthala details of architecture; current timings
  (sources list a short ~8:30-10:30 morning window — stated only as "short morning window");
  whether Murugan is called Dakshinamurthy here (one source, doubtful — not used); century
  of founding (7 used for the Tevaram era); Sambandar vs Sundarar attribution of the Ganga
  well/Jain expiation; Tevaram hymn counts.
