# Masilamaniswarar Temple, Vada Tirumullaivoyil (masilamaniswarar-vadatirumullaivoyil)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Thondai Nadu (Tevaram name: Tirumullaivoyil; "Vada" = northern, to
distinguish from Then Tirumullaivoyil near Sirkazhi). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Masilamaniswarar / Masilamani Easwarar; goddess **Kodiyidai Nayaki**
  (also given as Piraisoodi Amman in some sources — both names recorded; the goddess name in
  the brief, Kodiyidai Nayaki, is used as primary).
- Tevaram: sung by **Sundarar** (hymn addresses the Lord as "Pasupada Paranchudar" and treats
  His feet as the saint's true wealth). Listed as a Thondai Nadu Paadal Petra Sthalam
  (sources variously number it 22nd in Thondai Nadu; counts of 275/276 vary).
- Location: Thirumullaivoyal, Chennai–Avadi road, between Ambattur and Avadi, north-western
  Chennai (Tiruvallur district per administrative listings). Coordinates **13.1364, 80.1325**
  (Wikipedia: 13°08′11″N 80°07′57″E).
- Defining legend: King **Thondaiman** marched against two Kurumbar chieftains, **Vaanan and
  Onan** (some accounts add a third, Kanthan), who held the Puzhal forest region. His elephant
  became entangled in a dense **mullai (jasmine)** thicket; cutting the creeper, he found a
  linga that bled. In remorse he tried to take his own life; Shiva and Parvati appeared,
  stopped him and told him to build a temple; Shiva sent **Nandi** with him and he won. Name
  glossed *maasu-ila-mani* — "stainless/flawless gem" (the linga bled yet bore no fault).
  Goddess's name Kodiyidai ("creeper-slender waist") echoes the jasmine vine.
- Architecture: Dravidian; Chola character; entrance gateway/tower on the **north**, sanctum
  faces **east** (one blog source). Nandi reported as **not facing the Lord** (looking the
  other way) — commemorating Thondaiman's victory. Wall carving said to depict the king
  cutting the creeper to reveal the linga. Separate shrine for the goddess.
- Date: Wikipedia and other secondary sources say the temple was built by the **Cholas in the
  9th century CE**; 10th-century inscriptions reported incl. Parthivendravarman (feudatory of
  Uttama Chola; Battle of Takkolam 949 CE context). Used century 9, dynasty Chola.
- Worship/festivals: monthly Pradosham, Amavasya, Pournami, Kiruthigai (special puja days);
  special archana to Nandi, and **27 circumambulations by unmarried devotees** (marriage
  prayer); Chithirai festival (Apr–May); Maha Shivaratri.
- Access: Chennai–Avadi road; Tirumullaivayil railway station (suburban) ~1.5 km; Ambattur
  ~2 km; city buses, autos.
- Timings found (conflicting): 6:30–12:00 and 4:00–8:00; or pooja slots 6:00, 9:00, 18:00,
  20:30. Content says "confirm locally".
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinate (town/Wikipedia-level); text of the 10th-century
  inscriptions and the Pallava-vs-Chola attribution (a "Gaja-prishta" Pallava-style sanctum roof
  is claimed by one source only and is **omitted** from the content); the claimed interchange
  of Shiva/goddess shrine positions (uncorroborated, omitted); the Kurumbar chieftain names/
  number and the king's identity (one source identifies him as a son of Killi Valavan — omitted);
  the Erukku pillars from Kurumbur; exact Chithirai festival dates/details (known only from a
  page title); precise current timings; distance from the airport/Central.
