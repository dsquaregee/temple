# Ezhutharinathar (Aksharapureeswarar) Temple, Tiruinnambar / Innambur (ezhutharinathar-tiruinnambar)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tiruinnambar, திருஇன்னம்பர்). District: Thanjavur (village of Innambur, on the outskirts of Kumbakonam). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Ezhutharinathar (Sanskrit: Aksharapureeswarar); goddess Sugantha Kundalambikai
  (also written Sugandha Kundalambikai / Sugantha Kunthalambal), in a south-facing shrine on the
  right of the eastern outer courtyard. A separate shrine honours Nithya Kalyani, also called
  Soundara Nayaki (Wikipedia lists Nithya Kalyani as the consort; other listings give
  Sugantha Kundalambikai — both used in the entry).
- Tevaram: praised by Appar and Sambandar (shivatemples.com lists hymn counts; counts not
  confirmed, so not stated in the entry). Dinamalar lists it as the 46th Tevaram Shiva temple
  on the northern bank of the Kaveri.
- Coordinates: **~10.982, 79.339** (Wikipedia lists 10°58′54″N 79°20′20″E; village-level/temple
  point from Wikipedia infobox, not independently cross-checked).
- Defining legend: an honest accountant (named Sudharman in tradition; some sources spell it
  Sudhasman) was ordered by the king to present the realm's accounts by morning. Unable to
  finish, he took refuge in Shiva, who took his form, wrote and presented the accounts
  flawlessly; hence Ezhutharinathar ("Lord who knows letters/writing"). Further traditions:
  the sun god regained his strength here (one derivation of "Innambur"), Airavata bathed in the
  temple pond and worshipped, sage Agastya learned Tamil grammar from the Lord.
- Architecture: Dravidian; rectangular plan, two prakarams, ~1 acre; five-tiered east-facing
  rajagopuram; all shrines within rectangular walls; **no flagstaff (kodimaram)** per Wikipedia.
  Sthala vriksham: shenbagam (golden champak).
- Inscriptions: sources differ — one says Rajaraja I (as Rajakesari Varman) and a Vijayanagara
  chief (Kampannar); another names Koparakesarivarman. Entry says "reported to name Rajaraja I".
- Festivals: Aipasi Annabishekam (Oct–Nov); 10-day Purattasi Navaratri (Sep–Oct); Chithirai
  festival (Apr–May; sources vary, one says Kodabishekam); Margazhi Tiruvadhirai (Dec–Jan);
  Maha Shivaratri (largest crowds).
- Timings (sources disagree): Dinamalar 6–11 a.m. and 5–8 p.m.; shivatemples.com 7–12 and 4–8.
  Entry uses "roughly 6–11 / 5–8" and tells visitors to confirm.
- Access: ~10 km from Kumbakonam on the Tiruvaiyaru road; easily reached from Swamimalai;
  Kumbakonam is the nearest railway; Tiruchirappalli airport (~90 km, approximate).
- **Not verified:** founding dynasty/century of the structural temple (century 7 used for the
  Tevaram era; brick-shrine-predating-the-saints claim is a local tradition only); which Chola
  king the inscriptions name; hymn counts per saint; exact festival calendar; the practice of
  beginning a child's first lessons here (the "experience" text says only that it is a natural
  place to pray; no source confirms a formal Vidyarambham custom); Tiruchirappalli distance
  (approximate); exact temple coordinate; current timings; precise age (sources range from
  ~1,000 to 2,000 years).
