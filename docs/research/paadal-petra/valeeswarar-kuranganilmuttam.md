# Valeeswarar Temple, Kuranganilmuttam — research notes

Master-list row 244 (Thondai Nadu). Row facts confirmed: Valeeswarar; consort Iraiyar Valaiyammai (Irayarvalai Ammai); singer Sambandar.
Correction: the master list gives "Kanchipuram, Tamil Nadu"; the village is in Tiruvannamalai district (Cheyyar taluk), about 7–10 km south of Kanchipuram near the Palar, reached via Dusi (Thoosi) / Mamandur. Content uses Tiruvannamalai district.

- 6th Thondai Nadu sthalam (shivatemples tn06).
- Name: kurangu (monkey) + anil (squirrel) + kakkai/muttam (crow) — Vali (monkey) worshipped at the entrance, Indra (squirrel) from the south, Yama (crow) from the north; in one version they were under curses and freed by worshipping here. Panels depicting these are in the temple. Hanuman also said to have worshipped.
- Kaakkai Madu: nearby water body said to have been made when Yama as a crow pierced the earth with his beak for Shiva puja.
- Brahma worshipped here; old name Thiru-ilanathai (ecoheritage) — not used in content.
- Pallava rock-cut cave ("Kal mandakam") ~0.5 km north of the temple, attributed to Mahendravarman I (600–630) on style; no Pallava inscription found. Earliest inscription in the village: Rashtrakuta Krishna III (939–967), land grant; village said to be part of Pallavapuram. First reference to "Tirukkuranganilmuttam" in an inscription of Kulothunga III (1178–1218).
- Coordinates: NOT found in any source as a temple point. Estimated 12.770, 79.700 from: Wikipedia "Dusi, Tamil Nadu" (12°46′N 79°41′E) + village directory stating Kuranganilmuttam is ~2.3 km east of Dusi; consistent with "about 10 km south of Kanchipuram". Approximate — needs a map check.
- Festivals: not confirmed specifically; customary Shaiva observances, confirm locally.
