# Soundareswarar (Siddhanathar) Temple, Tirunaraiyur (soundareswarar-tirunaraiyur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri; 33rd of that group; counted as the 87th of the 276). District: Cuddalore (Kattumannarkoil taluk, Thirunaraiyur post, PIN 608303). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Soundareswarar / Soundaryeswarar, also **Siddhi Natheswarar / Siddhanathar**;
  consort **Thiripurasundari** (Tripurasundari). Seed hints confirmed.
- Tevaram name: **Tirunaraiyur** (திருநாரையூர்). Sung by **Sambandar and Appar** (Thirunavukkarasar)
  per Dinamalar/shivatemples/Wikipedia; one listing gives Appar 2 + Sambandar 1 pathigams. No
  Sundarar hymn found for this temple. Century used: **7** (Sambandar/Appar era).
- Coordinates: **approximate ~11.31, 79.60** (regional estimate from "17–19 km from Chidambaram on the
  Kattumannarkoil road via Kumaratchi"; Kattumannarkoil is 11.2727, 79.5555). **No sourced
  coordinate found — refine against OSM/Google.** Do not confuse with Tirunaraiyur near
  Kumbakonam (Thirunarayur Nambi Temple, a Vishnu shrine; a Thanjavur-district coordinate
  turned up in results and must not be used).
- Defining legends: (1) **Polla Pillaiyar** — self-manifested (swayambhu) Ganesha; correct Tamil
  spelling noted by one source as பொள்ளாப் பிள்ளையார் ("unchiselled"), the common
  பொல்லாப் பிள்ளையார் being a mis-spelling with a negative sense (content uses Pollappillaiyar /
  பொள்ளாப் பிள்ளையார்). Young **Nambiyandar Nambi**, temple priest's son, offered food; the
  image accepted after his devotion; King **Rajaraja I** tested it and Nambi sang the *Irattai
  Mani Malai* before the Lord accepted. Tradition: the Pillaiyar then guided the king to the
  Tevaram manuscripts in a chamber of the Chidambaram Nataraja temple; known as
  *Thirumurai Kanda Vinayakar*. Nambi's compilation fed the Tirumurai (hymns of Appar,
  Sambandar, Sundarar). Nambi's date given as 11th century. (2) Place name: a **Gandharva cursed
  by Durvasa to become a crane (naarai)** — Wikipedia; a tourism blog adds a Kasi-Ganga crane
  tale (folklore, not used).
- Architecture: east-facing three-tiered rajagopuram; on entry first a Vinayaka shrine, then a
  Nandi mandapam; shrines to Murugan, Gajalakshmi, Navagrahas, Sani, Bhairava, Surya;
  paintings of Nambi's life in the main prakara; images of Nambi and Rajaraja in the Pillaiyar
  sanctum (Wikipedia/directory listings). Content describes a separate Thiripurasundari shrine
  (consort always has a shrine at such temples; her exact position not sourced).
- Festivals: Vaikasi (May–Jun) Nambiyandar Nambi festival with Thirumurai chanting (a listing
  also mentions a 13-day Rajarajan festival — not used); Vinayaka Chaturthi; Navaratri; Skanda
  Sashti; Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham added as general Shaiva practice.
- Access: Chidambaram–Kattumannarkoil road via Kumaratchi, **17–19 km from Chidambaram** (sources
  differ); get off at Tirunaraiyur bus stop, ~500 m branch road to the temple. Listing timings:
  6:00–12:00 and 4:30–8:30, or 6:00–11:30 and 3:30–7:30 (sources disagree), so content gives
  general guidance only.
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinate; structural temple's founding dynasty/period (content
  says "Chola-era and later" generically; Nambi/Rajaraja tradition suggests Chola-era
  importance, but construction dates unsourced); exact verse/pathigam counts; goddess shrine
  details; whether the "Siddhi Vinayaka" label is used locally (sources use Polla Pillaiyar /
  Thirumurai Kanda Vinayakar); Nambi's compilation story is traditional and sources vary on
  details (the king's name appears as Rajaraja Abhaya Kulasekara in one source); current
  timings; nearest railhead distance and airports (Chidambaram and Puducherry / Tiruchirappalli /
  Chennai given as general options, not sourced); the Vaikasi festival's exact date and sponsor
  (sources differ).
