# Neelakandeswarar Temple, Tirumannippadikkarai (neelakandeswarar-tirumannippadikkarai)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri). District: Mayiladuthurai
(older sources say Nagapattinam). Tevaram name: Tirumannippadikkarai (திருமண்ணிப்படிக்கரை).
Tradition: Shaivism. Present-day village name: **Iluppaipattu** (Eluppaippattu), also written
Pazha Mannippadikkarai; postal address Manalmedu, PIN 609202.

- Deity: Shiva as Neelakandeswarar / Padikkarai Nathar (Thiruneelakandeswarar); consort
  **Amirthavalli** (also given as Amudhakara Valli; one source pairs the goddess
  **Mangalambikai** with the Padikkarai Nathar lingam).
- Tevaram: **Sundarar, one hymn** (Paadal Petra Sthalam, Chola Nadu north of the Kaveri).
- Name: "Padikkarai" = bank/landing; the temple is on the southern bank of the **Manniyaru**
  (also called the Subramanya river), hence "Pazha Mannippadikkarai." **Correction to seed
  hint:** the "Manni" is a river name, not "clay/mann"; no clay legend was found in sources.
- Coordinates: **approximate ~11.19, 79.59**, derived from sourced distances (~18 km from
  Mayiladuthurai, ~22 km from Sirkazhi, ~16 km west of Vaitheeswaran Koil); no published
  coordinate found. Refine against a mapping source.
- Defining legend: the Pandavas in exile found no lingas to worship; they lit lamps with
  iluppai (mahua) nuts/oil and meditated, and Shiva gave each a darshan as a linga — five lingas
  in separate shrines (Neelakandeswarar, Padikkarai Nathar, Mahadeshwara, Parameswarar and
  one more; sources disagree on the names and on which brother worshipped which). Second
  tradition: Duryodhana poisoned the Brahma Theertham; Parvati neutralised the poison and
  replaced it with nectar (shivatemples.com); another account says Shiva consumed the
  extracted poison to save the Pandavas. The tank is called Nachupoigai ("poison pond").
  Goddess honoured as Amirthavalli for the nectar.
- Architecture: east-facing temple; five lingas in separate shrines; goddess shrine; Brahma
  Theertham tank. One listing gives a five-tier rajagopuram, but this is not reliable (the
  same search also surfaced a different temple's five-tier gateway).
- Festivals (Dinamalar): 10-day Chithirai Brahmotsavam (Apr–May); Maha Shivaratri
  (Feb–Mar); Tirukarthikai (Nov–Dec). Pradosham is generic.
- Timings (sources differ): Dinamalar 7:00–11:30 and 15:30–20:00; shivatemples 6:00–11:30 and
  15:30–19:30; TN Temples Project 7–11:30 and 16–20.
- Access: ~18 km from Mayiladuthurai, ~22 km from Sirkazhi; buses from Mayiladuthurai and
  Vaitheeswaran Koil; ask for "Eluppaippattu". Nearest major railway Mayiladuthurai Jn
  (inference); nearest major airport Tiruchirappalli (inference).
- **Not verified:** founding dynasty/century of the structural temple (century 7 used for the
  Sundarar/Tevaram era; "Chola and later" is a generic assumption); exact coordinates; names
  and number of the sanctums and who worshipped which lingam; rajagopuram tiers; whether the
  goddess is Amirthavalli alone or also Mangalambikai; the east-facing entrance and the
  "modest enclosure" wording are generic (no source gives orientation or dimensions); the Mahabharata account is local
  tradition; current timings; nearest railway/airport.
- Search caveat: the Wikipedia/aanmeegam hits for "Neelakandeswarar" often return the
  separate temples at Tiruneelakkudi and Erukkattampuliyur; they were not used.
