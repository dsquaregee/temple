# Thiruketheeswaram Temple, Mannar (Mathottam) — research notes

Master-list row 193 (Eezha Nadu 2). Row facts kept; Eezha Nadu order not independently confirmed (see master notes). The goddess's name "Gowri" is the traditional name (Gowri Ambal / Gauri Ammai) — not confirmed by a search snippet in this pass; used as given in the master list.

- Place: Thiruketheeswaram, at Mathottam (Mantai / Manthai), the ancient port on the mainland opposite Mannar island, Mannar District, Northern Province, Sri Lanka. On the banks of the Palavi tank, near the estuary of the Aruvi Aru (Malwathu Oya).
- One of the 276 Paadal Petra Sthalams (two in Eezha Nadu: Koneswaram and Ketheeswaram). Sung by Thirugnana Sambandar and by Sundarar (Sundarar's pathigam refers to the banks of the Palavi). Sources date Sambandar to the 7th c.
- Deity: Shiva as Ketheeswarar (Ketheeswaranathar), lingam. Goddess: Gowri (traditional).
- Name: tradition says Ketu (the shadow planet) did tapas here and had the vision of Shiva and the Goddess — "Thiru-Ketu-Ishwaram".
- Legend: Mandodari, Ravana's queen, is said to have come from Manthai; her father Mayan, king of Manthai, is credited in tradition with building the ancient temple. (Present as tradition.)
- Counted among the Pancha Ishwarams of Sri Lanka (Wikipedia).
- History: largely destroyed by the Portuguese in 1575 (worship ceased; dates vary 1575–1589 across sources); stones reportedly reused for Mannar fort and other buildings. Site traced in 1894; rebuilt on its original site in 1903 after an appeal by Arumuka Navalar (1872), with support of a Chettiar donor and devotees; the present temple is the second shrine. (Keep neutral: say "destroyed in the sixteenth century during the colonial period" plainly, no polemic.)
- Palavi tank: ancient, restored; ablution in the Palavi on the morning after Maha Shivaratri is the great rite.
- Festivals: Maha Shivaratri (biggest; pilgrims from across Sri Lanka); annual Mahotsavam ending in Theertham at Palavi Theertham.
- Coordinates: 8.9147, 79.9561 — directory listing (8°54′53″N 79°57′22″E), consistent with Wikipedia location of the temple at Mantai, near the Palavi tank.
