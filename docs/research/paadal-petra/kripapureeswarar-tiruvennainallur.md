# Kripapureeswarar Temple, Tiruvennainallur (kripapureeswarar-tiruvennainallur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Nadu Nadu. District: Villupuram, Tamil Nadu. Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Kripapureeswarar (also Arutkondanathar / Thadutha-aatkondanathar);
  consort Mangalambigai (also Verkkanni Ammai).
- Coordinates: 11.860, 79.366 (Wikipedia, 11°51′37″N 79°21′59″E).
- THE defining Sundarar legend: on the saint's wedding day at Puttur, Shiva appeared as an
  old brahmin and claimed Sundarar (Nambi Arooran) as his hereditary slave, producing an
  ola (palm-leaf deed). The dispute went to a village assembly (panchayat) at
  Tiruvennainallur; the deed was verified, and the Lord led Sundarar here and vanished near
  the sanctum, leaving his padukas (footwear) — which remain in the temple. Here Sundarar
  began his Tevaram with the hymn "Pitha pirai soodi" ("madman who wears the crescent").
  Hence the Lord's name Thadutha-aatkondar — "he who stopped [the wedding] and took him as
  his own." Classified a Paadal Petra Sthalam.
- Place-name legend: Vennai Nallur ("butter town") — Parvati is said to have built a form
  of butter here during a tapas tied to Shiva's swallowing of the churned-ocean poison.
- History: present structure attributed to the Cholas (~10th c.), with a renovation placed
  in Uttama Chola's reign (971–988 CE). Sundarar is dated 8th–9th c. (century set to 8).
- Festivals: Aadi Swathi (Jul–Aug, 2-day Sundarar festival); Panguni Uthiram (Mar–Apr,
  10-day, flag-hoisting + float); Margazhi Tiruvadhirai / Arudra (Dec–Jan); Aavani Puttu
  (Aug–Sep); Skanda Sashti (Oct–Nov); Maha Shivaratri; monthly Pradosham.
- Not verified: exact goddess-name variants; festival dates (Tamil calendar — confirm
  locally); the precise extent of Chola vs later construction; transport distances.
