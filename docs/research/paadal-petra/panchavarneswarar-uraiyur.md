# Panchavarneswarar Temple, Uraiyur (panchavarneswarar-uraiyur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (south of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tirumukkeecharam / Thirumukkeecharam). District: Tiruchirappalli (Uraiyur is a quarter of Tiruchirappalli city). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Panchavarneswarar (also given as Mukkeesurathu Adigal / Thanthondreeswarar in listings); consort Kanthimathi Ammai (spelled Kantimathi / Gandhimathi / Kanthimathiyammai in sources; Kungumavalli also listed).
- Tevaram: sung by Sambandar (hymn 1.x; sources list Sambandar firmly; one Tamil source also lists Appar). Counted among the Paadal Petra Sthalams on the Kaveri's southern bank (Dinamalar: 5th on the south bank; tamilnadutemples.org numbers it 122nd of 276 in its own ordering).
- Coordinates: **approximate, locality-level ~10.829, 78.679** (Uraiyur, Tiruchirappalli). The Wikipedia temple page lists a coordinate (~10.367, 78.850) that does not fit Uraiyur and looks erroneous; it was not used. Exact temple point not confirmed — refine against a mapping source.
- Uraiyur: ancient capital of the early Cholas (Sangam-era Chola capital; Wikipedia's Uraiyur page cites Ptolemy). Also called Kozhiyur / Thirukozhi ("town of the cock").
- Defining legends (three traditions; versions differ):
  1. **Five colours**: the sage Uthanga (some tellings say Brahma) beheld Shiva here in five colours across five periods of a day, hence Panchavarneswarar.
  2. **Nagaraja's daughters**: Nagaraja the serpent king's five daughters each worshipped a linga on the bank of the Naga Theertham; a Chola king married the youngest; the lingas merged into one (some sources say six lingas). Commemorated by the Vaikasi festival.
  3. **Cock and elephant**: a Chola king (Karikalan in most sources; Veeravathithan in one Tamil source) on an elephant was saved/turned back when a cock struck the elephant's forehead; the king made the city his capital; origin of the name Kozhiyur. A further telling has the cock digging up a linga.
- History/dating: Chola-period inscriptions (one records the gift of the village Atigunakapaganallur; another mentions Rajasraya-Chaturvedimangalam in Uraiyur-kurram). Building commonly considered Chola with later additions; one source credits the 8th-century Pandya Varaguna II (contested); one source mentions Kochchenga Chola. Used century 7 for the Tevaram (Sambandar) era.
- Architecture: Dravidian; five-tiered rajagopuram (one source suggests it may be a recent renovation), three prakarams; dvarapalas at the sanctum entrance (one source: 8th century); koshta images of Brahma, Vishnu, Durga; Naga Theertham. Goddess in a separate shrine (assumed standard layout; separate-shrine detail not independently confirmed).
- Festivals: Vaikasi Brahmotsavam (May–June) commemorating the merging of the lingas; Maha Shivaratri; monthly Pradosham (generic, not site-specific). No Panguni festival found.
- Access: Uraiyur is ~5 km from central Tiruchirappalli (one temple database); reachable from Chatram bus stand; Tiruchirappalli Junction and Tiruchirappalli International Airport are the nearest rail/air. Divya Desam Azhagiya Manavala Perumal temple listed ~750 m away.
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinates; founding dynasty/date (used century 7); Tevaram hymn number and whether Appar also sang here; the "Kanthimathi" queen/midwife story that circulates on some pages (appears to be mixed up with the Rockfort Thayumanavar legend — not used); whether the goddess shrine is physically separate within the enclosure and the exact number of tiers of the gopuram; distances to junction/airport; current timings (sources conflict: 6–12 & 4–8 vs. six rituals 5:30 am–8 pm). A "Mukkeecharam = freedom from rebirth" belief appeared in one Tamil source and was not used.
- Seed-hint corrections: none to the deity/goddess; the Tevaram name and town were confirmed.
