# Idaiyattrunathar (Marundeeswarar) Temple, Tiruvidaiyaru (idaiyattrunathar-tiruvidaiyaru)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Nadu Nadu (Villupuram district). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Idaiyattrunathar, also called **Marundeeswarar** and Sivanandeswarar.
  The name Marundeeswarar is glossed generally as "Lord of medicine / the divine healer."
- Village: **Tiruvidaiyaru, now known as T. Edayar**, T. Edayar Post, Tirukkovilur Taluk,
  Villupuram District, Tamil Nadu. On the southern bank of the Then Pennai (South Pennar)
  river, ~5 km from Thiruvennainallur, on the Tirukkovilur–Tiruvennainallur–Arasur Road (SH 68).
- Coordinates: **~11.893, 79.337** (11°53′35″N 79°20′12″E per English Wikipedia infobox —
  temple/village level; treat as approximate).
- Paadal Petra Sthalam of Nadu Nadu; the hymn here is attributed to **Sundarar** (7th Tirumurai).
- Orientation: the presiding deity's sanctum **faces west** (unusual for a Shiva shrine);
  the goddess faces east, so Lord and consort face one another — read by tradition as a
  shrine for resolving obstacles to **marriage**.
- Legend (defining story): the sage **Suka / Sukabrahma**, bound by a curse, performed
  penance beneath the temple's **marutha tree** (Terminalia arjuna, the sthala vriksham)
  and was liberated by the grace of Idaiyattrunathar. "Suka" also means parrot, and the
  **parrots** about the temple are traditionally associated with him.
- Age/patronage: described as a Chola-era foundation with later additions; structure
  largely medieval/later.

## Consort name — important discrepancy
The task brief gave the consort as **"Elavarkuzhali."** This could not be verified for this
temple and appears to be an error: **Elavarkuzhali is the goddess of a *different* Paadal
Petra Sthalam — the Edaganathar temple at Thiruvedagam, Madurai district.** Every source
consulted for Tiruvidaiyaru / T. Edayar names the goddess here as **Sittridai Nayaki**
(spelling variants Sitridai / Sittridai Nayaki), with **Gnanambikai** given as an alternate
name in some listings. The content therefore uses **Sittridai Nayaki** (Gnanambikai noted as
a variant) and does **not** use Elavarkuzhali.

**Not verified:** exact temple coordinate (town/village level only); founding dynasty and
precise century of the structural temple (used century 7 for the Tevaram/Sundarar era —
one source calls the deity "revered in the 7th-century Tevaram"; another assigns the
masonry broadly to the Chola period, not firmly dated); a specific signature festival
(kept general — Maha Shivaratri, Pradosham, Panguni/Chithirai, car festival dates to be
confirmed locally); current timings; the "Lord of medicine" gloss of Marundeeswarar is a
general reading of the name, not a sourced legend specific to this village (the detailed
Agastya/herbs and Sun–Moon west-facing legends in sources belong to the *Thiruvanmiyur*
Marundeeswarar temple near Chennai and were deliberately NOT applied here).
