# Kutram Porutha Nathar (Aparadha Kshameswarar) Temple, Tirukkaruppariyalur (kutramporuthanathar-tirukkaruppariyalur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri). District: Mayiladuthurai
(older sources: Nagapattinam; Tamil Wikipedia lists it near Mayiladuthurai). Tradition: Shaivism.
Tevaram name: Tirukkaruppariyalur (திருக்கருப்பறியலூர்); modern village Thalainayar /
Thalaignayiru. Historic names: Karuppariyalur, Karmanasapuram; also called Melaikazhi (west
of Sirkazhi).

- Deity: Shiva as Kutram Poruttha Nathar / Aparadha Kshameswarar ("the Lord who forgives
  faults"); consort Kolvalai Nayaki (Tamil Wikipedia spells Kolvalai-Nayaki; English listings also
  give Vichitra Valayambigai / Vichitra Balambikai — not used).
- Tevaram: hymns by Sambandar and Sundarar (Wikipedia, Tamil Wikipedia). Sambandar's visit is
  recalled in the Periya Puranam (blog quoting Sekkizhar). Appar is **not** credited in any source
  found. Tamil Wikipedia numbers it 27th in the Kaveri north-bank list.
- Coordinates: **11.2117, 79.6558** (11°12'42"N 79°39'21"E, from Wikidata Q12984037). Note: the
  English Wikipedia page for "Thalainayar" (10.567, 79.767, near Vedaranyam) is a different place
  and was not used.
- Defining legend: Indra hurled his vajra at a being he did not recognise and it struck Shiva; on
  realising it, he repented and was forgiven - hence Kutram Poruttha Nathar / Aparadha Kshameswarar;
  tank = Indra Tirtham. Secondary: Hanuman and the sand linga of Sita (he coiled his tail around it
  trying to remove it; told to worship here to be freed of the offence; one source ties this to
  the nearby Kundala Karneswarar temple at Tirukkurakkaval). Third: Indrajit's aerial car halted
  over the tower until he bathed in the tank (dheivegam, single source).
- Architecture: east-facing swayambhu linga (Vikatan), goddess shrine facing south; "kokudi
  koil" type (templenet), sthala vriksha kokudi mullai (jasmine), tirtham Indra Tirtham (also
  Surya Pushkarani/Porthamarai in one list). Blog describes shrines of Vinayaka, Murugan and
  Dakshinamurthy around the sanctum and a single-tier brick vimana; valaitamil describes a
  raised/upper-floor arrangement (Uma Maheswarar on first level, Sattainathar on second), said to
  explain the "Melaikazhi" name. Sources differ; content stays general.
- History/inscriptions: about 12 inscriptions (Kulothunga III, Rajaraja III, a Vijayanagara
  ruler, "Pratapa Krishnadevaraya" per a blog); medieval place name "Rajathirajavalanattu
  Kazhumala Nattu Thaninayaka Chaturvedimangalam" - possible origin of "Thalainayar".
- Festivals (single-listing, dates not officially confirmed): Vinayaka Chaturthi (Aavani),
  Skanda Shashti and Annabhishekam (Aippasi), Thirukarthigai (Karthigai), Maha Shivaratri
  (Masi), Panguni Uthiram (Panguni). Monthly Pradosham is a general observance.
- Access: ~12 km from Mayiladuthurai (sources give 12-16), ~9-10 km from Vaitheeswaran Koil
  (nearest railway), Sirkazhi ~15-18 km; Mayiladuthurai-Manalmedu bus passes; buses from
  Vaitheeswaran Koil toward Thiruppanandal stop at Ilanthoppu, ~3 km short, no bus for the last
  stretch (one source says the temple is ~1 km off the main road). Nearest major airport:
  Tiruchirappalli (distance not verified).
- **Not verified:** founding date of the structural temple (century 7 = Tevaram era);
  gopuram/vimana form and dimensions; raised upper-shrine arrangement (Tamil source only);
  the Indrajit legend; the king Vichitrarangan childless-king legend (one source, omitted); the
  Vasishtha legend (omitted); the current renovation state of the temple (Vikatan reported
  disrepair/balalayam c. 2018; content only says it "may be" under renovation); timings (sources
  conflict, roughly 5-8 am to noon and 4-6 pm to 8-9 pm); precise festival dates.
