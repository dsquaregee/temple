# Thiruvanchikulam Mahadeva Temple, Kodungallur (thiruvanchikulam-mahadeva)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Malai Nadu / Kerala (Tevaram name: Tiruvanjaikkalam, திருவஞ்சைக்களம்).
Tradition: Shaivism. **The only Paadal Petra Sthalam in Kerala.**

- Deity: Shiva as Mahadeva (Thiruvanchikulathappan), installed image understood as
  Umamaheshwara / Sadashiva, facing east; consort Uma (Parvati as Umadevi).
- Sung by Sundarar (Sundara Murthi Nayanar) — "oldest reference in history in Thevaram hymns".
- Location: Kottappuram, just outside Kodungallur (Thrissur district; one source lists
  "Ernakulam" — Kodungallur is in Thrissur). Distance from town reported 2 km (pilgrimdata),
  2.7 km (Kerala Tourism sheet), ~6 km (Kerala Tourism Muziris page).
- Coordinates: **10.2103, 76.2066** (10°12′37″N 76°12′23″E, travelprofy). Same page also lists
  an erroneous 10.527, 76.215 (discarded). Kodungallur town centre: 10°14′02″N 76°11′41″E
  (Wikipedia). Seed hint 10.23, 76.20 refined.
- Defining legend: Sundarar and the Chera king Cheraman Perumal Nayanar (a close friend,
  per Periyapuranam, ~12th c. by Sekkizhar). Sundarar worshipped here at the end of his
  earthly life; Shiva sent a white elephant from Kailash on Adi Swathi day (July/Aug);
  Cheraman Perumal followed on horseback, whispering the Panchakshara into the horse's ear
  (Tamil blog account). Both reached Kailash from here. One account: on the way Sundarar sang
  a padigam that was sent back to Thiruvanchikulam at his request.
- Images/shrine of Sundarar and Cheraman Perumal in the temple. At the Aadi Swathi Guru pooja
  Sundarar is taken round on a white elephant with Cheraman on his horse (blog account).
- History: traditionally Chera period, 8th century (Kerala Tourism, dating also claimed
  >2000 years by Muziris Heritage / pilgrimdata — traditional, not archaeological).
  Built around the Kulasekhara capital of Mahodayapuram. Damaged in Tipu Sultan's invasion of
  Kerala (copper roofing, gold, jewels looted). Namaskara mandapam carries an 1801 renovation date.
  A local claim says part of the temple was built by Cheraman Perumal.
- Architecture: Kerala style (sloping copper/tiled roofs, no towering gopuram); 16-pillared
  Namaskara Mandapam before the sanctum; mural paintings of mythological scenes; ~33
  sub-shrines (incl. Shiva, Parvati, Ganapathi, Ayyappa, Hanuman, Sundaramurthy Nayanar).
- Festivals: annual festival in Malayalam month Kumbham (Feb–Mar) coinciding with Maha
  Shivaratri, with Anayottam (elephant race); Thiruvathira in Dhanu; Brahmotsavam in Vaikasi;
  Aadi Swathi (Jul–Aug). Palliyara pooja (Umamaheshwara / dampathi pooja) for marital harmony
  and children — Kerala Tourism says full-moon evenings, pilgrimdata says nightly.
- Administration: Cochin Devaswom Board (Kerala Tourism/pilgrimdata listing; not used in prose).
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinate (single source); true founding date (8th century vs
  "2000 years" claims); whether Cheraman Perumal himself built part of the temple; exact distance
  from Kodungallur (2–6 km); current timings; Palliyara pooja schedule; the 33 sub-shrines
  count (single source); the white-elephant/horse-mantra details are traditional (Periyapuranam
  tradition, blog accounts) — whether the padigam sent back is the one named in the Tevaram is
  not sourced; the "Mysorean invasion" damage date and extent; nearest railway station and
  airport distances (Kochi airport given as nearest international, no distance); bus/road
  details (Kochi–Thrissur coastal highway is general knowledge); Sundarar's shrine location
  within the complex; whether the sanctum faces east (one source only); Aadi Swathi procession
  currently observed in the form described (blog account).
