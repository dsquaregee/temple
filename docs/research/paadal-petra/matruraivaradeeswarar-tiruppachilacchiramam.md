# Matruraivaradeeswarar Temple, Tiruppachilacchiramam / Thiruvasi (matruraivaradeeswarar-tiruppachilacchiramam)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri). Tevaram name:
Tiruppachilacchiramam (திருப்பாச்சிலாச்சிராமம்; also Pachilachiramam / Tirupalasiramam /
Tiruppaachilaasramam). Town: Thiruvasi, Tiruchirappalli district (near Manachanallur).
Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Matruraivaradeeswarar (Mattruraivaradeswarar); consort **Balambigai**
  (Balambikai / Balasoundari).
- Tevaram: sung by both Sambandar and Sundarar (Wikipedia; Veludharan). Counted among the
  Paadal Petra Sthalams on the northern bank of the Kaveri (one source numbers it 62nd).
- Coordinates: **~10.8905, 78.6652** (Wikipedia: 10°53′26″N 78°39′55″E). Approximate;
  verify against a map before relying on it for navigation.
- Defining legend (as asked in the brief): **Kollimazhavan**, king/chieftain of the Kolli hills
  country (Mazha Nadu), had a daughter afflicted with the seizure illness called *muyalagan*
  (variously rendered epilepsy; one source says severe stomach ache, another snakebite venom);
  physicians failed. Learning that **Sambandar** was coming this way after worshipping at
  Thirumanthurai (Periya Puranam link per secondary sources), the king laid his daughter before
  the Lord (one source: before the Manikandeswarar sannidhi). Sambandar sang the decad beginning
  **"Thunivalar Thingal"**; the Lord turned the disease into a snake and danced on it (basis of
  the **Sarpa Nataraja**: snake under the lifted foot instead of Muyalagan, hair tied in a knot).
  **Conflict among sources:** tntemplesproject credits Sundarar with the cure (and notes
  "Muyalagan" may be a later interpolation); Dinamalar and others credit Sambandar. The entry
  follows the brief and the majority (Sambandar).
- Second tradition (Sundarar and the gold): needing gold to feed the poor, Sundarar asked the
  Lord and received silence, sang an angry hymn, found gold on the temple path, had it tested
  (one source: two goldsmiths who proved to be Shiva and Vishnu), and, ashamed of his doubt,
  changed his song to praise. Name *Matruraivaradar* is explained two ways: matru = change,
  urai = song/paean; or matru = quality, urai = rubbing on a touchstone.
- Architecture: east-facing; two 5-tier rajagopurams (Veludharan blog); sanctum, antarala,
  ardha mandapa, mukha mandapa; garbhagriha on a raised level in the central prakara; Dravidian,
  medieval Chola (one source suggests the core shrine may be much older — not used as a claim).
- Festivals: 11-day Brahmotsavam in Vaikasi (May–Jun); Tirukarthikai (Nov–Dec); Arudra
  Darshan (Margazhi); Thai Poosam; Maha Shivaratri; monthly Pradosham. (A Panguni Brahmotsavam
  turned up only for the unrelated Tiruvanmiyur temple — not used.)
- Access: Thiruvasi on the northern bank of the Kollidam, ~12 km from Chatram bus stand
  (shivatemples.com) / ~13–14 km from Tiruchirappalli (tntemplesproject); ~10 km from
  Tiruvanaikaval per shivatemples. Tiruchirappalli Junction (rail), Tiruchirappalli airport.
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinate (Wikipedia point only); the Tamil verse numbers of the
  Periya Puranam episode and which of the two saints is the cured-princess hero in every source;
  the Manikandeswarar shrine detail (single source, omitted from prose); founding dynasty and
  date of the structural temple (medieval Chola assumed; century 7 used for the Tevaram era);
  location of the Balambigai shrine within the plan; whether the goldsmith-as-Shiva-and-Vishnu
  detail is canonical (blog-level, kept out of the prose); current timings (sources conflict:
  8–12/5–8 vs 7–12/4:30–7:30); airport distance.
