# Amirthakadeswarar Temple, Tirukkadambur / Melakadambur (amirthakadeswarar-tirukkadambur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tirukkadambur, திருக்கடம்பூர்).
District: Cuddalore (Kattumannarkoil taluk), Tamil Nadu. Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Amirthakadeswarar (Sanskrit form Amritaghateshwara); consort Vidyujyothi Nayaki
  (spelled Vidyujothi Nayaki in some sources). Sthala vriksha: kadamba tree
  (*Neolamarckia cadamba*), from which the village takes its name.
- Tevaram: sung by **Appar and Sambandar** (sources agree on these two; Sundarar not attributed).
  The old village of Tirukkadambur later split into Melakadambur and Keezhakadambur; the
  Amirthakadeswarar temple is in **Melakadambur**. Keezhakadambur has a separate temple
  (Rudrakoteeswarar) — do not conflate.
- Coordinates: **village-level ~11.14, 79.31** (Wikipedia infobox for Melakadambur, 11°08′N 79°19′E).
  Exact temple point not found — refine against a mapping source. (Seed hint of 11.3, 79.5 was
  too far north-east.)
- Defining legends:
  1. **Nectar pot.** After the churning of the ocean the devas began drinking the amrita without
     first worshipping Vinayaka. He took the pot away; carrying it through the kadamba forest a
     drop fell and became a swayambhu linga. The devas apologised, Vinayaka returned the pot and
     told them to worship Shiva here as Amirthakadeswarar.
  2. **Chariot.** Aditi, mother of the devas, worshipped here daily; Indra, unwilling to see her
     travel, turned the temple into a chariot to carry it to Devaloka. Vinayaka (Aaravara Vinayaka)
     pressed the car with his foot and halted it; the left wheel remains sunk in the ground.
- Architecture: **Karakkoil** (chariot-form temple, one of very few surviving) — sanctum and vimana
  shaped as a processional car drawn by two horses on wheels; superstructure in granite. Wall
  panels: forms of Shiva, Ramayana, Krishna lila, Nayanmar stories. Separate shrines for the
  goddess, Murugan, Vinayaka (Aaravara Vinayaka).
- Date/patron: stone temple built in **1113 CE, 43rd regnal year of Kulottunga Chola I**
  (inscriptions of years 41 and 43 reported); probably replaced an earlier brick shrine.
  **Correction to seed hint:** the brief called it "early Chola"; sources attribute the
  present structure to Kulottunga I (late 11th–early 12th c.), so `century` is **12**, not 7
  (the Tevaram sung-era remains 7th c. and is noted in `period`).
- Festivals: Panguni 3rd–5th, sunrise (~6:15) sun rays on the linga for ~15 min with special
  puja; Maha Shivaratri (Masi); Aippasi annabhishekam; Arudra Darshan; Panguni Uthiram;
  monthly Pradosham. No Brahmotsavam found in sources.
- Timings (one listing, aanmeegam, ~480 days old): 7:30–9:30 am and 5:00–8:00 pm.
- Access: ~6–7.5 km from Kattumannarkoil; ~34 km from Chidambaram (major rail head).
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinates; distance to nearest airport/rail (stated generally as
  Chidambaram rail, Tiruchirappalli/Chennai air); current timings; dating discrepancy (one forum
  gives 1110 CE vs 1113 CE in Wikipedia); Rajendra Chola's Ganesha image from Vengi (blogs only,
  omitted); Vimana described as "Nagara" by one blog (omitted); sources disagree on whether the
  wheel/Indra legend vs amrita legend attach to which Kadambur/Kadavur shrine — the amrita-pot
  tale is also told of Thirukkadaiyur (a different temple), so details here follow the
  Melakadambur-specific accounts only; Tamil goddess name; photography and dress rules
  (general Tamil Nadu temple rules).
