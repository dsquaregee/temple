# Airavateswarar (Iyravatheswarar) Temple, Tiruvethirkolpadi (airavateswarar-tiruvethirkolpadi)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name:
Tiruvethirkolpadi / Tiru Ethirkolpaadi, திருஎதிர்கொள்பாடி). District: Mayiladuthurai (older
sources: Nagapattinam), Kuthalam taluk. Also called Mela (Melai) Tirumanancheri.
Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Airavateswarar / Iyravatheswarar; consort **Sugandha Kundalambikai**,
  Tamil Malarkuzhal Madhammai (Dinamalar: Malar Kuzhal Nayaki). Wikipedia lists the
  consort as Sugantha Kundalambikai.
- Tevaram: hymn(s) by **Sundarar** (the only saint attributed in the sources found); counted
  as the 24th Shiva temple on the north bank of the Kaveri (Dinamalar). The Periya Puranam
  does not single the shrine out. Distinct from the UNESCO Airavatesvara at Darasuram and
  from the other Airavata-named shrines (Nemam, Thirukottaram, Maruthuvakudi).
- Coordinates: **approximate, ~11.09, 79.58** (village level, between Kuthalam and
  Tirumanancheri). No sourced GPS point was found (Wikipedia pages located by search
  carried coordinates of other Airavata temples); refine against a mapping source.
- Defining legends: (1) *Ethirkolpadi* = "place of receiving/welcoming" (ethirkollal,
  receiving an honoured guest): after Sage Bharata (Bharadwaja in some accounts), as head
  priest, conducted the wedding of Shiva and Parvati at Tiruvelvikudi, he invited the couple
  to his place and welcomed them here with traditional hospitality; Shiva agreed to
  remain. (2) Airavata, Indra's white elephant, worshipped Shiva here to be freed of its
  sin/curse, so the Lord is Airavateswarar; Indra is also said to have regained his station.
- Architecture: **west-facing**, three-tier rajagopuram; sanctum, antarala and ardhamandapa;
  largely Chola-era structure — about **28 inscriptions, 11th–12th c. (Chola)** recorded
  (Wikipedia/related summary). Sequence of the wedding associations places it among the
  Tirumanancheri cluster (Pancha Krosha shrines near Kuthalam).
- Century: used **8** because the sources date Sundarar to the 8th century (one source
  reasons the temple may pre-date the 7th century; structural fabric is Chola, 11th–12th c.).
- Festivals: **none found.** Content uses only general Maha Shivaratri / monthly Pradosham
  and the proximity to Tirumanancheri / Tiruvelvikudi; it explicitly states that no
  calendar was found.
- Access: about 5 km from Kuthalam bus stand; buses from Mayiladuthurai to Kuthalam; near
  Tirumanancheri. Nearest major rail Mayiladuthurai; nearest major airport Tiruchirappalli
  (distance not sourced).
- **Not verified:** exact coordinates (approximate); Sambandar/Appar hymns (only Sundarar
  attributed); the festival calendar; the date of the earliest structure; the exact
  inscription count (~28, one source); whether Bharata and Bharadwaja are the same sage in
  this tradition (sources use both names); timings; whether the "west-facing" orientation
  is shared by every source (one source); distances other than "~5 km from Kuthalam bus
  stand"; the district spelling (Mayiladuthurai used; older listings say Nagapattinam).
