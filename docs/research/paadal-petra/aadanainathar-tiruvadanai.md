# Aadanainathar (Adhi Ratneswarar) Temple, Tiruvadanai (aadanainathar-tiruvadanai)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Pandya Nadu (Tevaram name: Tiruvadanai). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Aadanainathar / Aadi (Adhi) Ratneswarar; consort Snehavalli (Tamil: Anbayi
  Ammai). Tiruvadanai, Ramanathapuram district (older listings put it in Sivagangai or
  Madurai district; Ramanathapuram is better supported).
- One of the 14 Pandya Nadu Paadal Petra Sthalams; one pathigam by Sambandar. Rank in the Pandya
  sequence disputed (8th vs 9th) — prose says sources differ.
- Coordinates: **town-level 9.783, 78.919** (Wikipedia 9°47′N 78°55′10″E; post office
  9.7866, 78.9190). Exact temple point not found.
- Legend: Varuna, cursed by a sage to an elephant's body and a goat's head, worshipped here and
  regained his form; town name said to derive from it (aadu + aanai — the goat/elephant
  reading is an interpretation of the name). Variant: Vaaruni, son of Varuna, disturbed sage
  Durvasa's penance and was advised to pray at this temple.
- Features: ~10 acres; 9-tiered rajagopuram ~130 ft with stucco figures; Chokkatanchari
  mandapam of 291 carved pillars; tank Surya Pushkarini (vilwa theertham); sacred tree vilwa;
  further springs named for Varuna, Vaaruni, Markandeya, Agastya, Kamadhenu; major renovation
  credited to Bhaskara Setupati.
- Festivals: Brahmotsavam in Vaikasi (May–Jun); Ambal festival in Aadi; Arudra Darisanam;
  Karthikai Deepam; Kailasa Kaatchi (Sundarar's vision of Kailasa); Maha Shivaratri;
  monthly Pradosham. Sources disagree on festival lengths (10-day Vaikasi vs 15-day Aadi Pooram).
- Timings (listing a few years old): 6:00–12:00 and 4:30–8:30.
- Access: ~45 km from Karaikudi via Devakottai; buses from Karaikudi and Sivagangai;
  Tiruppunavasal ~21 km.
- **Not verified:** century/dynasty of the original or current structures (Pandya country only;
  Sethupathi renovation is undated; century 7 used for the Sambandar era); the exact temple
  coordinate; nearest railway/airport (not found — prose says so); the diamond-chariot claim
  (single source, omitted); the Bhaskara Sethupathi's regnal date; Pandya or Sethupathi
  inscriptions (searched, none found); Sundarar's connection to the Kailasa Kaatchi festival
  rests on one source; goddess name spellings; current timings.
