# Paalugandha Eswarar (Palunkanda Nathar) Temple, Tiruaappadi (paalugandhaeswarar-tiruaappadi)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tiruaappadi; also Tiruvaippaadi / Thiruvaipadi / Thiruvappadi). District: Thanjavur (Thiruvidaimarudur taluk). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Paalugandha Eswarar / Paalugandhanathar / Palunkanda Nathar ("one who relishes
  milk"; listed also as Aa-Paadiyaar); consort Periyanayaki (also listed Brihannayaki).
  Inscriptional names reported by a blog: Chokka Kooththar, Natarajar, Thiru Aappadi Udayar.
- Tevaram: sung by **Appar** (Thirunavukkarasar; Wikipedia cites Tevaram IV.48.5; shivatemples and
  tntemplesproject also list Appar only). Counted as the **40th** Shiva shrine on the northern bank of
  the Kaveri (Dinamalar), following Tiruppanandal (39th).
- Coordinates: **11.083, 79.4375** from the English Wikipedia geotag (11°04′58″N 79°26′15″E). Wikipedia
  states only that the village is near Thiruppanandal; the geotag may be village-level rather than the
  exact temple point. Roughly consistent with the ~2.5 km distance to Tiruppanandal and ~17 km to
  Kumbakonam. Refine against a mapping source.
- Defining legend: Visarasarman (the future Chandesa / Chandikeswarar, one of the 63 Nayanmars) was
  entrusted with tending cattle; he made a linga from the sand of the river Manni under an athi tree
  and bathed it with the cows' milk; his father kicked over the milk and the boy struck out; Shiva
  appeared and raised him to the rank of Chandesa. The standard Periya Puranam version has the
  boy's staff become an axe and cut off his father's legs; this note and the content text omit that
  detail ("struck out at him"). A second local tradition: a cowherd's milk spilled secretly at one spot,
  villagers dug and found a linga and worshipped it with milk, which the Lord accepted — hence
  Paalugandha. Place name from aa (cow) + paadi (pasture) → aa-aappaadi → Tiruaappadi.
  Tiruaappadi is treated as the place of Chandesa's liberation (mukti sthalam); his birthplace is
  said (tntemplesproject) to be Senganur/Chenganur, a nearby village — not used in the content text.
- Architecture: east-facing; two corridors (prakarams); no rajagopuram but an arched entrance;
  front mandapam in "vavval nethi" (bat's forehead) style, dated roughly 1400–1500 CE by one
  source; Periyanayaki shrine south-facing, to the right; Bhairava, Surya and Sani shrines in the
  mahamandapa; Chandikeswarar shrine close to the sanctum (one source says also in the garbhagriha
  itself, plus a north shrine); an athi (sacred fig) tree in the south-west corner of the inner
  prakaram with a linga beneath it marking where Chandeswarar worshipped
  (shivatemples nc40). Tirtham: Manniyaru (river) / Athi Theertham (temple directory).
  Reported (blog, unverified): rebuilt in stone in the Chola period, extended by Pandyas and
  Vijayanagara; hence "Chola-period stone temple" in `period`.
- Festivals: Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham as the main observances; Chandikeswarar Brahmotsavam on
  the new-moon day after Maha Shivaratri, approx. Feb–Mar (Tamil Maasi) — the sources do not name the
  month precisely, so the content says "around February to March".
- Access: Kumbakonam–Anaikarai route; ~2.5 km from Tiruppanandal; ~17 km (tntemplesproject) to
  ~18 km (shivatemples) from Kumbakonam; temple set back from the main road, auto from
  Tiruppanandal suggested (shivatemples). Nearest major airport Tiruchirappalli (~100 km, estimate).
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinates; century/dynasty of the present structure and the
  Pandya/Vijayanagara additions; the inscriptional names; the 15th-century date of the front
  mandapam; Chandikeswarar's position in the sanctum; sthala vriksham (athi tree is described only as
  a sacred fig in the inner enclosure); the Brahmotsavam date and whether the temple has other major
  festivals; current timings (listings differ: 7–11 and 17:00–19:30 per Dinamalar vs 8–9 and 16–18
  per shivatemples); distances; airport distance. Century 7 used for the Appar (Tevaram) era.
