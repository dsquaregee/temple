# Neelivaneswarar (Gneelivaneswarar) Temple, Tiruppainjeeli (neelivaneswarar-tiruppainjeeli)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri; Tevaram name: Thiruppainjeeli). District: Tiruchirappalli, Tamil Nadu. Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Neelivaneswarar (Gneelivaneswarar, Neelakandeswarar; also Sorudiya
  Eswarar / Chotrudai Eswarar in his shrine); consort Visalakshi (Neelanedunkanni).
- Tevaram: praised by the Tevaram saints (Appar associated with the meal legend).
- Coordinates: **approximate ~10.94, 78.65** (village near Manachanallur, ~15-20 km north of
  Tiruchirappalli). Exact temple point not found in sources — refine against a mapping source
  when possible.
- "Gneeli" = a variety of plantain; the place is remembered as an ancient grove of these.
  Tradition calls it the southern Kailash.
- Legend (the temple's defining story): After Shiva struck down Yama to save the boy
  Markandeya at Tirukkadaiyur, the world fell out of order; Bhudevi, Vishnu, Brahma and the
  gods begged for Yama's restoration. Shiva revived him at Tiruppainjeeli, Yama emerging through
  an opening in the earth on Thai Poosam. The Yama shrine shows him as a child beneath the
  feet of Shiva and Ambica with Murugan (Somaskanda form). Second legend: Shiva appeared to the
  weary, hungry Appar as a Brahmin, fed him, guided him to the temple and revealed himself as a
  linga, worshipped as Chotrudai Eswarar ("Lord of the Meal").
- Architecture: east-facing, three-tiered rajagopuram; a second unfinished (mottai) gopuram;
  cave-style sanctum ~6 ft below the courtyard with a two-tiered vimana; nine steps from the
  second gopuram honoured as the Navagrahas (no separate Navagraha shrine); shrines of
  Visalakshi, Vishnu as Senthamarai Kannan, Yama, and Chotrudai Eswarar. Cave sanctum
  traditionally attributed to Mahendravarman I (c. 640 CE per Wikipedia; tradition, not a firm
  record); Rajaraja I and Rajendra I inscriptions show early 11th-century endowment.
- Festivals: Thai Poosam (special worship of Yama); ten-day Chithirai Brahmotsavam; Maha
  Shivaratri; monthly Pradosham; Appar Guru Puja; longevity prayers for 60th and 80th birthdays.
- Access: ~15-20 km north of Tiruchirappalli via Manachanallur; buses from Chathiram stand;
  Uttamarkoil nearest railway (a few km); Tiruchirappalli International Airport ~28 km;
  Divya Desam temple of Tiruvellarai ~4 km away.
- **Not verified:** the Mahendravarman attribution (tradition only); exact temple coordinate;
  the exact Navagraha-steps count and Yama-shrine construction date (sources differ);
  current timings (listings differ; entry says early morning to ~midday and late afternoon to
  ~8 pm).
