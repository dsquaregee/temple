# Pataleeswarar (Padaleeswarar) Temple, Thirupathiripuliyur (Cuddalore) — research notes

Master-list row 233 (Nadu Nadu). Row confirmed: Pataleeswarar / Periyanayaki (Arundhavanayaki, Brihannayaki); Cuddalore; Appar and Sambandar.

- Place: Thirupathiripuliyur, the old-town neighbourhood of Cuddalore; temple ~0.5 km from Tiruppadiripuliyur railway station (Wikipedia).
- Paadal Petra Sthalam, Nadu Nadu (shivatemples nn18). Thevaram: Thirunavukkarasar (Appar) 1 pathigam, Sambandar 1 pathigam (shivatemples). Name: Pathiri (patali, trumpet-flower tree) + Puliyur (tiger — Vyaghrapada). One of the "Puliyur" shrines of Vyaghrapada (Chidambaram = Perumpatrapuliyur).
- Moolavar: Pataleeswarar / Padaleeswarar; also Karaiyetreeswarar (the one who brought Appar ashore — tntemplesproject). Consort: Periyanayaki / Brihannayaki / Arundhava Nayaki.
- Sthala vriksham: Pathiri (patali) tree; an ancient "Adi Pathiri" tree within the prakaram.
- History: Wikipedia says built in Pallava and medieval Chola periods; Appar "believed to have adopted Saivism at this temple". Tradition identifies the town with the Pataliputram of the Periya Puranam, where Appar (as Dharmasena) lived in a Jain monastery. Inscriptions not confirmed — omitted.
- Legend of Appar: on Pallava king Mahendravarman's order Appar was tied to a stone and cast into the sea; singing the Namasivaya pathigam he floated ashore near here (place called Karaiyeravittakuppam / shown to visitors as the spot). Not fully confirmed in multiple sources — phrased as tradition.
- Legends: Vyaghrapada and Patanjali worshipped here; Goddess did penance (Arundhava Nayaki = she of rare penance) — tradition.
- Architecture: east-facing, five-tier rajagopuram (Grokipedia — moderately reliable; used cautiously).
- Festivals: Vaikasi brahmotsavam reported on temple sites (not confirmed) — use Shivaratri, Chithirai/Appar observance generically; confirm locally.
- Coordinates: 11.7484, 79.7486 — Wikidata Q7144202 (11°44′54.09″N 79°44′55.06″E) and Commons category, via search text. Distinct from Devanatha, Thiruvahindrapuram (11.7447, 79.7089) and Vamanapureeswarar, Thirumanikuzhi (11.7374, 79.6836).
