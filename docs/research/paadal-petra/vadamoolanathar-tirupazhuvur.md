# Vadamoolanathar (Alanthuraiyar) Temple, Tirupazhuvur (vadamoolanathar-tirupazhuvur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri). Village: Keezhapaluvur (Kilapazhuvur / Keezhapalur), Ariyalur district, Tamil Nadu — the historic Paluvettaraiyar country. Tevaram name: Tirupazhuvur (திருப்பழுவூர்). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as **Alanthuraiyar (Aalanthuraiyaar)**, also called Vadamoolanathar / Vadamooleswarar / Yogavaneswarar; consort **Arunthavanayagi** (Arundhavanayagi; also Yogathapasvini / Mahathapasvini). **Seed-hint correction:** the temple's principal name in the sources is Alanthuraiyar; Vadamoolanathar is the alternate name — both kept in the entry (name "Vadamoolanathar", Alanthuraiyar given in deity, history and legends).
- Tevaram: sung by **Sambandar** (confirmed). No source found for Appar or Sundarar hymns here, so only Sambandar is stated. (Vallalar and Arunagirinathar are said to have praised it — not used.)
- Coordinates: **11.0418, 79.0682 (approximate)** — taken from the Kilapaluvur post-office point (pin.codes.in); Wikipedia's Keezhapalur village entry is 11°02′50″N 79°04′14″E (~11.047, 79.071), about 0.8 km away. The exact temple point was not found; refine against a mapping source.
- Location: ~12 km south of Ariyalur on the Ariyalur–Tiruvaiyaru road.
- Defining legends:
  - Name from the banyan (pazhu / aal; Pazhuvur, Alanthurai); temple tree is the *pazhu aal*; place once a banyan forest.
  - **Parasurama** did penance here to be freed of the sin of killing his mother at his father Jamadagni's command; tanks Brahma Tirtham and Parasurama Tirtham; Lord sought for relief from brahmahatti dosha.
  - **Parvati's penance** gives the goddess the name Arunthavanayagi; a local/blog telling (not firmly sourced) has her cover Shiva's eyes in play, do penance, and be taken as consort — marked in the prose as "a local telling".
  - Prayers for reunion of separated partners (hindu/temple-info sources).
  - Panguni Brahmotsavam: on day 3 the Lord visits Melapazhuvur to give darshan to Jamadagni, returning at night. Tradition that the sun worships the Lord on the 18th of Panguni with its rays.
- Architecture: east-facing; **three-tiered rajagopuram**; two prakarams; vimana described as eight-faced (sources disagree on Dravida vs. Vesara — "Dravidian" used for style; the prose says only "eight-faced"); Gajasamharamurti in the koshtam; 23 inscriptions, mostly Chola (Wikipedia / tntemplesproject).
- History: brick original rebuilt in stone under **Parantaka I and Uttama Chola** (sources also say renovation in Sundara Chola's time — not used). Ariyalur district portal: the place formed part of the capital of the **Paluvettaraiyar** chieftains (Perumpazhuvur). Inscription mention of Paluvettaraiyar Kandan Amudanar's victory at Vellore in Parantaka's 12th year (not used in the prose). Tevaram antiquity: the temple "existed in the 7th century" per Sambandar's pathikam, hence century 7.
- Festivals: Panguni Brahmotsavam (Mar–Apr); Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham (generic, as at every Shiva temple — not specifically sourced for this temple).
- Access: Ariyalur nearest railway town; Tiruchirappalli the nearest airport (qualitative; distance not verified).
- **Not verified:** whether Appar/Sundarar also sang here; exact temple coordinate; gopuram height; Parantaka I vs. later rebuild attribution (sources differ); the Paluvettaraiyars' marital/Chera ties and their connection to Sundara Chola's ministers (a forum source only, not used); the Panguni 18 sun-worship detail (single source); the "covering the eyes" folk story (blog); Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham specifics for this shrine; current timings and festival dates; the number of inscriptions beyond the 23 reported.
