# Amravaneswarar Temple, Maanthurai (amravaneswarar-tirumaandurai)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tirumaandurai,
திருமாந்துறை). District: Tiruchirappalli (village of Maanthurai / Mandurai / Manthurai, about 3 km
before Lalgudi on the Tiruchirappalli–Lalgudi road). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Amravaneswarar (Aamravaneswarar), "Lord of the Mango Grove"; consort
  Azhagammai, also given as Balambikai / Valambika. Some sources also call the Lord
  Mrukandeeswarar. The seed hint "Mandurainathar" was **not found** in sources and is not used.
- Counted as a Paadal Petra Sthalam on the north bank of the Kaveri (listed as no. 58 in the
  north-bank sequence by one source). Sambandar sang it; Wikipedia credits Appar too
  (a Tripadvisor snippet says three of the four saints — not corroborated, not used).
- Coordinates: **approximate, village-level ~10.875, 78.779.** Sources disagree: Wikipedia infobox
  10.85, 78.867 (that longitude falls east of Lalgudi, inconsistent with "3 km before Lalgudi");
  Tripadvisor embedded data 10.875, 78.779 used as the more plausible. Refine against a map.
- Name: "maa" (mango) + "thurai" (ford/bank); the site remembered as an ancient mango forest
  (Aamravana = mango forest).
- Legend (defining): per Wikipedia, Shiva gave a deer a mango, hence the village name. TN Temples
  Project: a sage cursed to be reborn as a deer is freed by Shiva, who gives the young deer a
  mango tree; the sage asks Shiva and Parvati to stay, and they remain as Amravaneswarar and
  Balambikai. A blog notes a deer worshipping Shiva under a mango tree is shown in stucco in the
  temple. Secondary tradition: sage Mrigandu worshipped here for a son, Markandeya, and Mandurai is
  said to be Markandeya's birthplace (TN Temples Project); Adi Sankara is also said to have
  worshipped here (blog, not used in the prose).
- Architecture: Dravidian; east-facing **three-tiered rajagopuram**; sculptures of Ganesha and
  Murugan flank the main entrance; main prakara holds the east-facing sanctum of Amravaneswarar;
  temple tank. Chola kings greatly expanded the temple in the 11th century (Wikipedia); Chola
  stone reconstruction (blog). Goddess shrine direction is **conflicting** (north-facing per one
  blog, south-facing per another) — direction omitted from the entries.
- Festivals: Chithirai festival, 15 days (April–May), dramatising incidents of the legend; six daily
  rituals and twelve yearly festivals (Wikipedia). Maha Shivaratri / Pradosham are generic
  (assumed, standard for Shiva temples).
- Access: ~15 km from Tiruchirappalli on the Trichy–Lalgudi road, ~3 km before Lalgudi; Tiruchirappalli
  is the nearest main railhead and airport city. Timings reported ~7:00–11:30 and 4:30–7:30
  (shivatemples.com).
- Century 7 used for the Tevaram era (Sambandar/Appar); structural expansion 11th c. (Chola).
- Note: a different temple, Akshayanathasamy at Thenkarai Manthurai near Suriyanarkoil, shares a
  similar name — not this temple.
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinates; direction the goddess shrine faces; whether Appar
  sang here (Wikipedia says yes, other sources only name Sambandar); the "Mrigandu / Markandeya"
  tradition and Adi Sankara association (single-source); exact distances to railway/airport;
  current timings; the standing of the generic Maha Shivaratri/Pradosham observance here;
  seed hint "Mandurainathar".
