# Palvannanathar (Paulvannanathar) Temple, Tirukazhippalai (palvannanathar-tirukazhippalai)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tirukazhippalai / திருக்கழிப்பாலை). District: Cuddalore (present temple at Sivapuri, beside Tirunelvoyal, near Chidambaram). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Palvannanathar ("Lord of the milk-white hue"); consort Vedanayaki (all sources agree).
- Tevaram: sung by Sambandar, Appar and Sundarar (shivatemples.com hymn counts: 2 / 5 / 1); Arunagirinathar (Thiruppugazh) also listed as praising it. Counted among the Paadal Petra Sthalams on the northern bank of the Kaveri (Kaveri Vada Karai).
- Coordinates: **~11.371, 79.714** (Wikipedia: 11°22′15.5″N 79°42′50.8″E, the present Sivapuri temple). Source-level, not independently re-checked on a map.
- Defining legend: sage Kapilar made a lingam of white sand in the forest by the river; a king's horse struck it with its hoof and left a mark; the sage, thinking it unfit, began making another; Shiva and Parvati appeared and stopped him, explaining the sand was white because the divine cow Kamadhenu had poured her milk over the whole place, so the lingam was already hallowed. Hence the name Palvannanathar (pal = milk, vannam = colour). Devotees believe milk poured in abhishekam is absorbed by the image. Sources differ on the lingam's fate (tntemplesproject: the marked lingam is the sanctified one; aanmeegam: the struck lingam fell into the river and Kapilar's original remained unaffected). The content text tells the common core and notes that tellers differ.
- History / relocation: original temple on the north bank of the Kollidam (Coleroon), ~13 km SE of Chidambaram (and ~5 km from Karaimedu per tntemplesproject); damaged by floods; the deity and attendants relocated to a new temple at Sivapuri, near Tirunelvoyal.
- Architecture: three-tiered rajagopuram; ~1 acre; concentric rectangular walls; shrines to Surya, Vinayaka, Subramanya, Vishnu (Mahavishnu), Mahalakshmi, Navagrahas, Chandra, Kala Bhairava; Bhairava image said to resemble the one at Varanasi (temple also called a Bhairava temple); Ambal shrine beside the mandapam, facing south; sthala vriksha vilva; theertham the Kollidam river.
- Festivals: Vaikasi Brahmotsavam (May–June) is the most prominent; Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham are general.
- Access: ~4 km from Chidambaram bus stand (shivatemples.com; Dinamalar says ~3 km) on the Kavarapattu route; Tirunelvoyal ~300 m (one source); nearest railway Chidambaram; Chennai the major airport (~200+ km by road).
- Corrections to seed: the seed named "Kudikadu" as the town; sources place the *present* temple at **Sivapuri (beside Tirunelvoyal), Cuddalore district**, and give Karaimedu (not Kudikadu) as the original washed-away site. Kudikadu was not found in any source and is not used in the content. Seed said "Paulvannanathar"; sources use Palvannanathar (Paulvannanathar kept as a variant in the English deity line).
- **Not verified:** the founding dynasty/century of the present structure (medieval Tamil patrons used generically; century 7 for the Tevaram era); the exact temple coordinate; current timings (generic text used); the sanctum/goddess orientation beyond "Ambal faces south" (one aggregate summary of the Wikipedia article); whether Kala Bhairava worship days (Ashtami) are specially kept (stated in general terms); the specific festival days of the Brahmotsavam; the precise road distance from Chidambaram (3–4 km by different sources); a Wikipedia mention of "Oondreswarar and Minnoli Amman" as prominent shrines appeared to belong to a different temple and was NOT used.
