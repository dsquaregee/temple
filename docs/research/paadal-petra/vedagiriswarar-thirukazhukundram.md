# Vedagiriswarar Temple, Thirukazhukundram (vedagiriswarar-thirukazhukundram)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Thondai Nadu (Tevaram name: Tirukkazhukkunram / திருக்கழுக்குன்றம்). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Vedagiriswarar (the hilltop linga, "Lord of the Veda-hill"); consort
  Tripurasundari (Thiripurasundari Amman), whose shrine is the foothill temple.
- Counted among the Paadal Petra Sthalams: hymns by Tirugnana Sambandar, Appar (Tirunavukkarasar)
  and Sundarar; Manikkavasagar also associated. Thondai Nadu region.
- Coordinates: **~12.6067, 80.0625** (Wikipedia gives 12°36′24″N 80°03′45″E). Town centre /
  temple area; refine against a mapping source for the exact sanctum point.
- Defining identity — "Pakshi Tirtham" (bird-theertham): for centuries a pair of birds
  (described as eagles; most likely Egyptian vultures) were said to arrive at the hilltop
  shrine at noon each day to be fed by the priest. Tradition holds the birds were sages
  under a curse, and that a pair worshipped here and attained liberation in each of the four
  yugas. The daily visits are said to have ceased in recent decades.
- "Vedagiri" = "the hill that is the Veda." Legend: a sage (named as Bharadwaja in some
  accounts) sought to master the Vedas; Shiva revealed that the hill itself embodies the
  Vedas — learning is a fistful of soil beside the mountain — so devotion, not accumulated
  learning, grants liberation. Some tellings make the hill(s) stand for individual Vedas.
- **Sangu (Sankha) Theertham** — the tank at the eastern entrance. Tradition: once every
  twelve years a conch (shangu) emerges naturally from its waters, collected and preserved
  in the temple. Origin tied to Markandeya, to whom Shiva gave a conch for his rituals.
- Architecture: twin complex — a **foothill temple** to Tripurasundari with four gopurams
  (likened to the Annamalaiyar temple's), east-facing seven-tiered rajagopuram; and a
  **hilltop sanctum** of Vedagiriswarar reached by ~565 stone steps. A **Pallava cave shrine**
  on the hill path is dated c. 610–640 CE; the temple's origins are traced to the Pallavas,
  with later Chola and Vijayanagara additions.
- Festivals: 10-day Chithirai festival; monthly **Pournami girivalam** (full-moon hill
  circumambulation) drawing large crowds; Aadi Pooram; Maha Shivaratri; monthly Pradosham.
- Access: Thirukazhukundram, Chengalpattu district, Tamil Nadu; on the Chengalpattu–
  Mahabalipuram road, ~14 km from Chengalpattu, ~10 km from Mahabalipuram, ~70 km south of
  Chennai. Nearest railway Chengalpattu Junction; nearest airport Chennai.
- **Not verified:** the Sanaka–Sanandana attribution of the bird-sages (sources name other
  pairs by yuga, not Sanaka/Sanandana — kept general); exact sanctum coordinate; precise
  step count (565 per one source; others differ); founding century of the structural temple
  (Pallava cave c. 7th c. used for century 7); current timings. The town is also listed under
  Kancheepuram district in some older sources.
