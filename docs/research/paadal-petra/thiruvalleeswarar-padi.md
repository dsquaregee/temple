# Thiruvalleeswarar Temple, Padi (thiruvalleeswarar-padi)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Thondai Nadu (Tevaram name: Tiruvalithayam). District: Chennai (Padi, north-west Chennai). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Thiruvalleeswarar / Valleswarar / Valithayanathar; consort Jagadambika
  (Jagathambikai).
- Tevaram: praised by Tirugnana Sambandar; ranked variously as the 20th Paadal Petra Sthalam of
  Thondai Nadu or the 21st Thondai Nadu Tevaram shrine, depending on source.
- Coordinates: **locality-level ~13.097, 80.187** (Padi). Exact temple point not found in
  sources — refine against a mapping source when possible.
- Legend (the temple's defining story): the sage Bharadwaja, born as a small bird (*valiyan*,
  glossed as a sparrow in some sources), worshipped a linga here and was made chief of the birds,
  giving the name Valithayam. Second tradition: Brihaspati (Jupiter) was freed of a curse by
  bathing in the temple tank and worshipping, on Markandeya's counsel — hence Guru parihara
  sthalam, honoured as Chennai's Guru temple, with a Guru shrine in the courtyard.
- Architecture: east-facing, three-tiered gopuram; **gajaprishta (apsidal) vimana** over the
  sanctum; goddess in a south-facing shrine; sacred trees padiri and kondrai; tank Bharadwaja
  Theertham. Structural temple of the Chola period (~11th century) with later additions;
  administered by the state HR&CE department.
- Festivals: Chithirai Brahmotsavam (Apr–May); Guru Peyarchi (Jupiter transit); Thursdays;
  Thai Krithikai (Jan–Feb); Maha Shivaratri; monthly Pradosham.
- Access: Padi, off Avadi Road near the TVS/MTC junctions; walk ~100 m from the Padi Lucas-TVS
  bus stop towards Padavattamman temple, then the perpendicular road opposite. City buses and
  suburban rail serve the area.
- **Not verified:** exact founding century of the structure (Chola, ~11th, from existing en
  entry); exact coordinate; bird identity (valiyan/sparrow); the medieval damage-and-restoration
  tradition; current timings (local listings roughly 6:30 am–12:00 and 4:30–8:30 pm).
