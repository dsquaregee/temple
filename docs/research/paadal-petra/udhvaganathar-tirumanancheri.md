# Udhvaganathar (Kalyanasundareswarar) Temple, Tirumanancheri (udhvaganathar-tirumanancheri)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri). District: Mayiladuthurai
(some sources still say Nagapattinam, the pre-2020 district). Tradition: Shaivism.
Tevaram name: Tirumanancheri (திருமணஞ்சேரி).

- Deity: Shiva as Udhvaganathar, also Kalyanasundareswarar (the beautiful bridegroom);
  consort Kokilambal (written Kokhila/Gokhila in some English sources). A Tamil source also
  gives her Tevaram-style name Yazhin Menmozhi Ammai. The seed hint "Kuyilinum Iniyaal" was
  **not found** in any source consulted; not used in the content.
- Tevaram: hymns by Appar and Sambandar (one decad each, per Tamil Wikipedia-derived listing).
  Numbered 25th in the Chola Nadu north-bank list on Wikipedia (numbering varies by source).
- Coordinates: **approximate ~11.107, 79.571** (secondary mapping/travel sources, 11°6′25″N
  79°34′16″E; Kuthalam ~3-4 km east). Not a surveyed temple point; refine when possible.
  Note: the 11°05′N 79°34′E coordinate that surfaced in search belongs to the Thiruvelvikudi
  Kalyanasundareswarar temple, a different shrine (and a different Tevaram sthalam).
- Defining legend (the marriage temple): Parvati asked at Kailasa to wed Shiva again on
  earth; he agreed without naming time or place; after an incident she was sent to earth in
  cow form and, after penance, restored; at Tirumanancheri Shiva appeared as Kalyanasundarar
  and wed Kokilambal; the seven seas came to witness (Saptasagara tirtham / the moat).
  Name derives from manam (marriage); "Udhvaha" = marriage. Sources vary on the details of the
  cow-form episode (one places her release at Thiruvaduthurai) - content kept general.
- Marriage worship: devotees garland the Lord and perform archana, take the garland home,
  and return with the old garland after the wedding (Dinamalar). A daily Thirukalyanam ritual
  and a blessed lemon given to devotees are reported by one site (AstroVed-type sources).
- Sacred features: tirtham Saptasagaram; sthala vrikshams karu-oomattai (dhatura), vanni,
  konrai.
- History/architecture: per Wikipedia, brick temple c. 7th century, rebuilt in stone around
  the 4th regnal year of Parantaka I; 2 inscriptions of Parantaka I (year 4), 3 of Rajaraja I
  recording a gift by Sembiyan Mahadevi (mother of Uttama Chola; one Tamil source says she was
  the queen of Gandaraditya); about 28 inscriptions in all, including Kulothunga III, Pandya,
  Hoysala (Veera Ramanatha) and Nayak records. Dravidian, east-facing, separate goddess shrine.
- Festivals: **Chithirai Thirukalyanam** (3 days, April-May, Poosam star); on the eve the
  Lord goes as bridegroom to **Edirkolpadi**, where the priest receives him as father of the
  bride with purna kumbha honours, then he returns to wed (Maalaimalar/Dinamalar). Also Aadi
  Pooram, Tiruvadhirai, Tirukarthigai (Dinamalar); Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham added as
  general observances.
- Access: Thirumanancheri village, ~5 km from Kuthalam on the Kumbakonam-Mayiladuthurai
  road; sources give 26-32 km from Kumbakonam. (A source claiming ~40 km from Mayiladuthurai is
  inconsistent with the coordinates; Mayiladuthurai is ~10-12 km by road, not stated in content.)
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinate; gopuram height/tiers and enclosure size; the
  attribution of the rajagopuram to Mallappa Nayak (single Tamil source); the claim that
  there is no Navagraha shrine and that the goddess sits alone in bridal pose (single source;
  only the bridal grace is used softly); the specific roles of Brahma (priest) and Vishnu
  (giver of the bride) at the wedding (popular but not found in searches - omitted); the
  Kamadeva-revival legend (mentioned in one source, omitted); the Thiruvaduthurai cow-form
  location; current timings; distance to Tiruchirappalli airport (stated only as "a few hours
  by road").
