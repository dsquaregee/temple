# Tiruthalinathar Temple, Tirupputtur (tiruthalinathar-tirupputtur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Pandya Nadu (counted 6th of the Pandya Nadu Tevaram shrines;
Tevaram name Tiruputhur / Tiruttali). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Tiruthalinathar / Tiruttaleeswarar (swayambhu linga); consort Sivakami
  (also Sivakamasundari / Soundarya Nayaki).
- Location: Tirupputtur (Tiruppattur), Sivagangai district, between Madurai and Karaikudi;
  ~22 km from Karaikudi, ~27 km from Sivagangai, ~60 km from Madurai.
- Coordinates: **town-level ~10.119, 78.611** (average of the Wikipedia town point ~10.13, 78.62
  and the Tirupattur bus-stand point ~10.108, 78.602). Exact temple point not found in sources —
  refine against a mapping source when possible.
- Legends (as tradition): (1) Sage Valmiki did penance in a kondrai forest until an anthill
  (puthu) formed round him; Shiva appeared beside it — origin of "Tiruputhur". (2) Mahalakshmi
  wished to see the Lord's dance; Shiva showed her the Gauri Thandavam (also called Lakshmi
  Thandavam); she worshipped here, and the name Tiruthalinathar is traditionally tied to this.
- Architecture: east-facing gopuram, three enclosing prakarams, pillared mandapam before the
  sanctum, goddess shrine north of the main sanctum, separate Tirunageswarar shrine in the
  outer (third) prakaram, west-facing Yoga Bhairavar shrine. Teerthams named: Thali and
  Sivagangai (one source also lists Sridhali).
- History/inscriptions: chiefly a Chola-age structure with later Pandya and Chera additions and
  maintenance; inscriptions record lamp gifts and perpetual-lamp endowments; the village is
  described as a Brahmadeya village in Koluvur kurram. The Maruthu Pandiyar brothers are named
  as a patron in one source (not used in prose).
- Festivals: Chithirai Bhairavar festival (Apr–May); Karthikai Champaka Sashti (Nov–Dec);
  Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham assumed as at all Shiva temples.
- Access: Madurai (~60 km) nearest major airport; Karaikudi nearest sizeable railway town (bus
  access from surrounding towns).
- **Not verified:** which of the three Tevaram saints sang this shrine (Sambandar and Appar are
  named by most listings; one source also names Sundarar — prose says only "the saints of the
  Tevaram"); exact construction date/dynasty (century 7 used for the Tevaram era);
  exact temple coordinate; timings (sources conflict — 5:30/7:30 to 12:30 and ~4–5 to 8:00/8:30 pm
  variously reported); the Dinamalar "1,000–2,000 years" age estimate (not used); the Bhairavar
  shrine's history (only its existence and festival are used).
