# Sakshinatheswarar (Satchinadeswarar) Temple, Tirupurambayam / Thirupurambiyam (sakshinatheswarar-tirupurambayam)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tirupurambayam, திருப்புறம்பயம்). District: Thanjavur (Kumbakonam taluk; PIN 612303). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Sakshinatheswarar / Satchinadeswarar / Satcheeswarar / Punnaivananathar;
  consort **Karumpadu Solliammai** (the brief's spelling). Sources vary widely on the consort's
  name: Karumbanna Solli / Karumbanna Sollammai, Ikshuvani, Kuraivilla Azhagi (Wikipedia). The
  entry uses Karumpadu Solliammai per the brief; Tamil rendering கரும்படு சொல்லம்மை.
- Tevaram: praised by Sambandar, Appar and Sundarar (one pathikam each per shivatemples.com);
  also Arunagirinathar (Thiruppugazh) per one listing.
- Coordinates: **approximate ~10.99, 79.31** (village area near Kumbakonam). Sources conflict:
  Wikipedia lists 10°29′N 78°41′E (clearly inconsistent with the stated ~9–11 km from
  Kumbakonam, likely an error); the pincode site lists 10.7789, 79.1400 for the post office
  (~33 km from the Wikipedia point, also doubtful). The value used is a rough village-level
  estimate, **needs refinement against a map source**.
- Defining legend (sakshi = witness): a young woman of a Madurai merchant family was married
  here to a kinsman with only a vanni tree, a well and the Lord as witnesses (some tellings add
  the kitchen/madappalli where the couple stayed). When the man denied the marriage, Shiva went
  to Madurai with the witnesses and testified — the last of the 64 Tiruvilaiyadals in
  Paranjothi Munivar's Tiruvilaiyadal Puranam. The vanni tree is said to stand in the north-east
  corner of the Madurai Meenakshi Sundareswarar complex. **Variants:** one account has Shiva
  reviving a snake-bitten groom; a Perichikoil version has Sambandar reviving him; another
  version has a first wife disputing the marriage. The entry follows the common core only.
  Second legend: at the deluge (pralaya) Vinayaka drew the waters of the seven seas into a well
  here and saved the village (the "puram-payam"/outside-the-flood name explanation).
- History: the **Battle of Thirupurambiyam (c. 879/880 CE; one source c. 885)** — Pallava king
  Aparajita Varman with Western Ganga Prithvipati I and Chola prince Aditya I against Pandya
  Varaguna II (Wikipedia battle article: alliance victory). Aditya I received Thanjavur c. 885;
  later ended Pallava rule (~897). Wikipedia's village article says Aditya I built a temple here
  in remembrance, naming it Aditeshwaram — repeated in the entry only as "one tradition says".
- Architecture: two gopurams; western is tallest, five storeys, ~72 ft (22 m); Brahma
  theertham; sthala vriksham punnai (a separate vanni tree stands in the precinct, not the
  sthala vriksham). Present masonry largely Nayak (16th c.); goddess shrine credited to
  Rajaraja I (Wikipedia). Vinayaka image anointed with honey on Vinayagar Chaturthi; honey
  said to be fully absorbed. Temple maintained by Madurai Adheenam (one source; not used in
  entry).
- Festivals: Vinayagar Chaturthi (honey); Maha Shivaratri and monthly Pradosham (generic).
- Access: ~11 km from Kumbakonam per one listing, 9.3 km per another; railway at Kumbakonam
  (~8 km per another source); about 5 km off the Kumbakonam–Swamimalai main road;
  Tiruchirappalli airport (~90 km, approximate).
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinate (see above); consort's name spelling; full festival
  calendar (no source found; entry says so); current timings (none sourced, generic text used);
  who revives/what happens to the groom and the exact list of witnesses (variants); the
  Aditya I / Aditeshwaram temple tradition; consort-shrine attribution to Rajaraja I; century of
  the original structure (century 7 used for the Tevaram era); Tiruchirappalli distance.
