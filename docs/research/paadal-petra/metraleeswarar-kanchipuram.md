# Metraleeswarar (Metralinathar) Temple, Kanchipuram (metraleeswarar-kanchipuram)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Thondai Nadu (Tevaram name: Thirumetrali). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Metraleeswarar / Metralinathar, a west-facing granite linga. Consort given
  as Metralanayagi (Parvati) on Wikipedia; Dinamalar notes the goddess has her own east-facing
  shrine — unusual in Kanchipuram, where Kamakshi is traditionally the common Mother Goddess for
  the city's Shiva temples.
- Counted a Paadal Petra Sthalam. Tevaram hymns sung by the saints; sources disagree on which:
  Wikipedia says **Appar and Sambandar, one verse each** (First Tirumurai); Dinamalar's listing
  instead credits Tirunavukarasar (Appar) 1 and Sundarar 1. Prose kept general ("the Tevaram
  saints"); the Sambandar name-legend is the one all sources attach to this shrine.
- Name legend (defining story): as Shiva turned **west — merku in Tamil —** to hear the child-saint
  Sambandar's hymns, the place became Thirumetrali and the deity Metralinathar ("Lord of the
  western shrine"). Secondary legend: Vishnu did penance here and melted into a linga at the power
  of the hymns, remembered as **Odavurugeeswarar / Odha Urugeeswarar** ("the Lord who melted and flowed").
- Location: **Pillayarpalayam** quarter, northern part of old Kanchipuram.
- Architecture: sanctum faces **west** (unusual); **three-tiered east-facing rajagopuram**;
  separate shrine for the goddess. Compact Dravidian plan. (Architectural survey / dating not found.)
- Festivals: **Thirugnana Sambandar Gurupuja**; **Panguni Uthiram** (Mar–Apr); Maha Shivaratri;
  monthly Pradosham. Administered by the Tamil Nadu HR&CE board.
- Access: in old Kanchipuram, ~75 km from Chennai (nearest international airport); well connected
  by rail and road.
- **Coordinates: town-level ~12.843, 79.703** (Pillayarpalayam / Kanchipuram area). Exact temple
  point not found in sources — refine against a mapping source when possible.
- **Not verified:** founding dynasty/century of the structural temple (used **century 7** for the
  Tevaram/Sambandar era; dynasty left general — Pallava/Chola-era patrons not firmly sourced);
