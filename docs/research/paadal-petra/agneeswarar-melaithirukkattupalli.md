# Agneeswarar (Theeyadiappar) Temple, Melai Tirukkattupalli (agneeswarar-melaithirukkattupalli)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (south of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Melai Tirukkattupalli, மேலைத் திருக்காட்டுப்பள்ளி). District: Thanjavur. Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Agneeswarar (also Theeyadiappar), a swayambhu linga, modest in size, topped with a
  five-hooded serpent; consort Soundaryanayaki (also Soundara Nayaki / Azhagammai) per Dinamalar.
- Tevaram: sung by Sambandar and Appar (shivatemples lists one pathigam each). Distinct from
  Keezhai Tirukkattuppalli (Aranyasundareswarar, near Tiruvenkadu — already in the catalog); "Melai" =
  western/upstream.
- Coordinates: **town-level ~10.848, 78.956** (Tirukkattupalli post office, pin.codes.in lists
  10.847 N / 78.956 E; latitude.to ~10°50′39″N 78°57′23″E). Wikipedia's infobox value (10.51, 78.57)
  is inconsistent with the "15–18 km west of Thiruvaiyaru" location and was not used. Exact temple
  point not found — refine against a mapping source.
- Defining legend: Agni carried the sins of all offerings burnt in yagnas and scorched whatever he
  touched; at Shiva's direction he bathed in the temple water (Agni Teertham) and worshipped here, was
  cleansed, hence Agneeswarar / "Agneeswaram". Agni Teertham survives in the form of a well.
- Secondary tradition (versions differ — used in prose as "several versions"): a king of Uraiyur and
  sevvanthi flowers meant only for Shiva (from Sage Saroma's garden in one telling) given to his
  queens; when the offering was misused sand rained on Uraiyur until the king/queen repented at
  Tirukkattupalli. Wikipedia gives a variant in which a Chola queen of Uraiyur took refuge here from a
  sandstorm and Shiva relieved her of the heat. Who gave the flowers differs between sources.
- Architecture: five-tiered gopuram; three concentric corridors; sanctum at a subterranean level
  (devotee descends). On the southern bank of the Kudamurutti, ~15–18 km west of Thiruvaiyaru.
- Festivals: Panguni Peruvizha (annual brahmotsavam, Mar–Apr), Masi Magam, Maha Shivaratri, Aipasi
  Annabishekam, Margazhi Tiruvadhirai; monthly Pradosham added as generic. (One source also mentions
  Karthigai Sundays — not used.)
- Timings (sources): 6:00–11:00 and 16:00–20:30; written as "about … confirm locally".
- Access: Tirukkattupalli PIN 613104; ~24 km from Thanjavur (town panchayat profile); nearest railway
  Budhalur ~8 km (town profile), Thanjavur Junction main railhead; Tiruchirappalli ~45 km west, nearest
  airport. Reported to be **under renovation** (Wikipedia / tntemplesproject).
- **Not verified:** founding dynasty / century of the present structure (used century 7 for the
  Tevaram era); exact temple coordinate; the goddess's shrine position and any specific inscriptions;
  the meaning of "Theeyadiappar" (not glossed in the prose); the road distance to Tiruvaiyaru (sources say
  15 or 18 km — written as a range); current timings and renovation status; the festival list is from
  temple-directory sites, not the temple's own calendar; Tevaram attribution (Sambandar + Appar) rests
  on directory sites and Wikipedia, verses not checked.
