# Vilvavananathar (Vilvavaneswarar / Vilwaneswarar) Temple, Tiruvaikavur (vilvavananathar-tiruvaikavur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tiruvaikavur / Vaigavur). District: Thanjavur (Kumbakonam taluk, PIN 612301). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Vilvavananathar / Vilvavaneswarar / Vilwaneswarar; consort Valaikkai Nayaki
  (also Valaikai Nayagi; alternate name Sarvajana Rakshaki). Sacred tank: Yama Theertham.
- Tevaram: Wikipedia says praised by Appar, Sundarar and Sambandar; one shivatemples-derived result
  lists only Sambandar. Content therefore says only "the Nayanar saints / Tevaram hymns."
- Coordinates: **10.9942, 79.2864** (Wikipedia: 10°59'39"N 79°17'11"E). Sourced, not
  independently checked against a map.
- Name / vilva forest: the vilva is Shiva's favoured tree; Wikipedia says the presiding deity is
  named Vilvavaneswarar because Shiva rose as a vilva tree during a great deluge.
- Defining legend: a hunter chasing a deer enters a sage's hermitage; the sage, refusing to
  give the deer up, takes tiger form (in another telling Shiva himself was the tiger); the hunter
  climbs a vilva tree and drops leaves through the night, which fall on a linga below; it is
  Maha Shivaratri night, so he unknowingly worships Shiva; next day Yama comes for him and
  Nandi / Shiva's guardians / Dakshinamurthy drive Yama out. Temple regarded as where Shivaratri
  originated. Yama dug the tank and bathed the linga (Dinamalar-based summary).
- Architecture: Dravidian; Wikipedia describes a five-tiered rajagopuram (one photo caption
  elsewhere says three tiers — content says "a rajagopuram" without a tier count). Yama Theertham
  directly opposite the main entrance. Nandi (all Nandi images per tntemplesproject) faces the
  entrance rather than the sanctum, locally explained as keeping Yama out. Dakshinamurthy in a
  standing posture with a staff (single source; phrased as "described" in the content).
- Festivals: Maha Shivaratri (Masi, Feb–Mar), 2-day festival with Theerthavari on new-moon day and
  a night palm-leaf-cart procession (Panchamukha); crowd figure ~2 lakh reported (Wikipedia /
  Dinamalar). Wikipedia lists Masi Magam, Panguni Uthiram; also Vaikasi Visagam, Adipooram,
  Navarathri, Annabishekam, Theerthavari as prominent.
- Access: ~17 km from Kumbakonam by road (shivatemples, Dinamalar, via Swamimalai ~7 km),
  Thanjavur ~45 km. Wikipedia says "8 km north of Kumbakonam" and tntemplesproject says 15 km;
  content uses the 17 km road figure. Kumbakonam nearest railway.
- **Not verified:** founding date/dynasty (the Chola/7th-century attribution is traditional;
  century 7 used for the Tevaram era); gopuram tier count (5 vs 3); the standing-Dakshinamurthy
  detail; which Tevaram saints sang here (see above); exact timings (none sourced; generic
  wording used); that the Valaikkai Nayaki shrine is a separate shrine (content says only that she
  is honoured as consort); Tiruchirappalli as nearest major airport (general knowledge; distance
  not sourced); Pradosham not mentioned in content. The "Vyaghrapada / Sambu / Kaundinya" names
  floated in the seed hints were **not found** in any source and were not used.
