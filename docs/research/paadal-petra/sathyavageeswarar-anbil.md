# Sathyavageeswarar (Satyavageeswarar) Temple, Anbil (sathyavageeswarar-anbil)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Chola Nadu (north of the Kaveri) (Tevaram name: Tiruanbilalanthurai / Anbilalanthurai). District: Tiruchirappalli (Anbil, near Lalgudi, on the bank of the Kollidam). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Sathyavageeswarar (also Sathyavaheeswarar / Brahmapureeswarar); consort
  **Soundara Nayaki** (Soundaranayagi). Swayambhu linga in an east-facing sanctum.
- Tevaram: one pathikam each by Sambandar and Appar (shivatemples.com). Counted as the 57th
  Shiva temple on the northern bank of the Kaveri (Dinamalar); a blog gives "111th" Paadal
  Petra sthalam overall — counts differ by source, not used.
- Coordinates: **~10.85, 78.867** (Wikipedia infobox for the temple, 10°51′N 78°52′E).
  Approximate; the nearby Vishnu temple (Sundararaja Perumal) is listed at 10.8678, 78.8822.
  The village article's coordinate (11°16′N 78°31′E) is ~40 km off and was not used.
- Name: the name is read as "Lord of true speech" (satya + vak + isvara). Local tradition
  (blog sources): the sage **Vageesar** sought Shiva's blessing here → Satyavageeswarar;
  **Brahma** (from Satyaloka) worshipped here → Brahmapureeswarar. The caller's seed hint of a
  "sathyavak" legend was not found as a distinct story; the above is the sourced explanation.
- Defining legend: **Sambandar** found the Kollidam in flood and sang his hymn from the far
  bank; Shiva had sent the flood to test him; the temple's Ganesha leaned forward to hear —
  **Chevi Saitha Vinayakar** ("the Vinayaka who inclined his ear"). One account: Chola king
  Paranthaka settled Sama Veda (Jaimini) brahmins to chant before him — "Sama Veda
  Vinayakar". Another source says Sambandar came here after worshipping at Tirukkanur.
- History/architecture: oldest masonry Chola, ~9th century; later work up to the Thanjavur
  Nayaks (16th c.); inscriptions of Cholas, Thanjavur Nayaks and Thanjavur Marathas;
  five-tiered gopuram; shrines to Saptamatas, Bhikshatanar, Kasi Viswanathar and Visalakshi,
  Bhairava, Murugan, Navagrahas. Sthala vriksha: banyan (Dinamalar); tank: sources differ
  (Chandra Theertham per Wikipedia; Gayatri Theertham per Dinamalar) — not named in content.
- Anbil plates / Aniruddha Brahmarayar: the Anbil plates of Sundara Chola (4th regnal year)
  are among the earliest Chola copper-plate grants (land to Aniruddha Brahmadhirajan).
  Sources tie them variously to the Vishnu temple (Sundararaja Perumal) and the village;
  one source links Aniruddha Brahmarayar with inscriptions of this Shiva temple. The content
  therefore says only that the village is known for them and that sources differ.
- Festivals (Dinamalar): Margazhi Tiruvadirai (Dec–Jan), Masi Maha Shivaratri (Feb–Mar),
  Panguni Uthiram (Mar–Apr); Wikipedia: five annual festivals, unnamed. Pradosham generic.
- Access: ~8 km east of Lalgudi, ~20 km from Tiruchirappalli (temple guides); timings
  reported 6:00–11:00 and 17:00–20:00 (shivatemples.com). The "Anbilalanthurai" etymology
  (alam = banyan) is not stated in sources and was not used.
- **Not verified:** exact temple coordinates; century of the original (Tevaram-era) shrine
  (century 9 used for the oldest surviving masonry, per Wikipedia — other Wave C entries use
  the Tevaram century, so this may be normalised); the Brahma/Vageesar legend (blog-level
  sources); Paranthaka/Sama Veda detail (single source); how the Anbil plates relate to the
  Shiva temple; train/bus specifics (rail station at Lalgudi not confirmed); current timings;
  Tevaram verse numbers.
