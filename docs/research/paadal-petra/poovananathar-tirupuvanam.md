# Poovananathar (Pushpavaneswarar) Temple, Tirupuvanam (poovananathar-tirupuvanam)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Pandya Nadu (one of the 14 Pandya Nadu Tevaram shrines; one
listing numbers it 11th; Tevaram name Tiruppuvanam). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Poovananathar / Pushpavaneswarar (self-manifested linga, described as
  three-faced); consort Soundaranayaki, also called Minnanayal (Dinamalar). The brief named her
  "(Minakshi)" — not found in the sources for this temple, so "Minnanayal" is used.
- Location: Tirupuvanam (Thirupuvanam), Sivagangai district, on the Vaigai (southern bank per one
  source); ~18–20 km from Madurai on the Rameswaram/Sivagangai road, ~29 km from Sivagangai
  (a Tamil Nadu temples-project map lists 24 km to Madurai; the range 18–24 km varies by route).
- Coordinates: **temple point 9.827, 78.257** (Wikipedia, 9°49′37″N 78°15′24″E). The town point
  (~9.855, 78.274) sits ~3 km away and was not used.
- Tevaram: tradition/sources say hymns by all three saints (Appar, Sambandar, Sundarar); one
  source calls it the only Pandya Nadu sthalam of the 14 with hymns by all three.
- History: stone structure attributed to Aditya I (870–907 CE); other accounts say late 9th or
  early 10th century core with later additions funded by Sembiyan Mahadevi; Aditya's lamp
  endowments, Uttama Chola (970–985) grants for worship, a Raja Raja I-era village gift;
  patronage also credited to Cheras and Pandyas. Wikipedia also gives a "7th century Chola"
  origin — inconsistent, so the earliest date is left uncertain in the prose.
- Legends (as tradition): one of the 64 Tiruvilayadal sites (ch. 36 of the Tiruvilayadal
  Puranam, alchemy episode) — the dancer Ponnaiyal and the Siddha/Shiva who turned vessels to
  gold for her golden image; Sambandar would not enter for fear of treading on sand he saw as
  lingas, and Shiva had Nandi lean aside; local belief that the Vaigai turns north here before
  flowing east, hence the comparison with Varanasi.
- Architecture: seven-tiered gateway tower; Soundaranayaki in a separate shrine to the left of the
  mahamandapam with her own entrance and small tower. Teerthams: Vaigai and Manikarnikai.
- Festivals: Aadi Mulaikottu (Jul–Aug, Dinamalar); Puratasi (Sep–Oct) bathing rituals mentioned
  once; Maha Shivaratri and Pradosham assumed as at all Shiva temples.
- **Not verified:** the Chithirai festival (searched, not confirmed — not used); exact timings
  (conflicting: 6:00–11:00 am / 4:00–8:00 pm vs 6:30 am–12:00 pm / 4:30–8:00 pm); the "three-faced
  linga" and "Soundaranayaki shrine to the left" details rest on single listings (Wikipedia /
  templefolks); exact century of the earliest shrine (century 7 used for the Tevaram era);
  Madurai distance (18–24 km quoted variously).
