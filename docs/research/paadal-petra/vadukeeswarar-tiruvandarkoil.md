# Vadukeeswarar (Panchanadeeswarar) Temple, Tiruvandarkoil (vadukeeswarar-tiruvandarkoil)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Nadu Nadu. Tradition: Shaivism. **In Puducherry
Union Territory** (not Tamil Nadu) — the village sits on the Villupuram–Puducherry road.

- Deity: Shiva as Vadukeeswarar, also Panchanadeeswarar / Panchanatheeswarar, and
  Vadugurnathar. Consort Tripurasundari (Thiripura Sundari).
- Praised in the Tevaram by **Tirugnana Sambandar**; his padigam is in the **First
  Thirumurai**. Classed as a Paadal Petra Sthalam of the Nadu Nadu group.
- Coordinates: Wikipedia gives **11°55′08.7″N 79°39′26.3″E → ~11.919, 79.657**
  (temple point, reasonably precise). Village also called Vadugur; PIN 605102.
- Legend (the temple's defining story): the demon **Mundasuran** won boons and tormented
  Brahma and the devas. Shiva commanded **Vaduka Bhairava**, one of the eight Bhairavas,
  to destroy him; afterward Vaduka Bhairava worshipped here to be freed of the sin of the
  killing, and the Lord took the name **Vadukeeswarar**. Hence special Bhairava worship,
  esp. on **Karthigai Ashtami**. Town name read from Vadukur / Andar Koil → Tiruvandarkoil.
- Architecture: east-facing; concentric rectangular **granite walls**; sources say it has
  **no towering gopuram** (Wikipedia notes a flat entrance tower); sanctum reached via
  **mahamandapam + arthamandapam**; Tripurasundari shrine in the second precinct (one
  source: faces north). **Protected monument under the ASI.**
- History: built by the **Cholas**; inscriptions of later Chola kings incl. **Rajaraja
  Chola I and Kulottunga Chola I**. Century set to **7** for the Tevaram/Sambandar era.
- Festivals: **Maha Shivaratri** (Masi, Feb–Mar); **Vaikasi Visakam** (May–Jun); **Skanda
  Sashti** (autumn); special **Bhairava** rites on Karthigai Ashtami.
- Access: on the **Villupuram–Puducherry highway**, ~21 km E of Villupuram, ~20 km W of
  Puducherry town; Villupuram is the nearest major railway junction.

- **Not verified:** precise orientation of the Tripurasundari shrine (north vs. second
