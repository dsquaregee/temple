# Thirumakaraleeswarar (Maakaraleeswarar) Temple, Tirumagaral (adaikalam-katha-nathar-tirumagaral)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Thondai Nadu (Tevaram name: Tirumagaral / Thirumaagaral, திருமாகறல்). Tradition: Shaivism.

- **IMPORTANT naming correction:** the seed hint "Adaikalam Katha Nathar" was **not found** in any
  source for this temple (Wikipedia, shivatemples.com index, Tamil Wikipedia, temple blogs). The
  presiding deity at Tirumagaral is **Thirumakaraleeswarar (Maakaraleeswarar)**, also called
  **Udumbeeswarar** (Lord of the monitor lizard). The slug (`adaikalam-katha-nathar-tirumagaral`)
  was kept as assigned; the content uses the researched names.
- Deity: Shiva as Thirumakaraleeswarar / Udumbeeswarar; goddess **Tribuvananayagi**
  (Tamil: திரிபுவனநாயகி; "Tribhuvaneswari" in another source). Not "Tripurasundari" (that is Marundeeswarar, Chennai).
- Tevaram: hymn (padigam) by **Sambandar**; tradition calls it a "vinai-neekkum padigam"
  (hymn that removes the burden of past deeds). Counted 7th of the Tevaram shrines of Thondai Nadu.
- Coordinates: **approximate ~12.705, 79.745** (Magaral village, on the Kanchipuram–Uthiramerur
  road via Orikkai; ~16 km from Kanchipuram, ~12 km from Uthiramerur). Derived from those
  distances; no verified temple point found. The seed 12.95/79.80 is too far north. A pin-code
  listing (631603) gave 12.925, 80.199, which conflicts and was rejected.
- Defining legend: a king (usually Rajendra Chola; one source suggests Vijayalaya Chola) chased a
  golden monitor lizard (udumbu) into an anthill and seized its tail; blood flowed from the
  mound, he fainted, and a divine voice said the lizard was Shiva and told him to build a temple.
  The linga is lizard-tail shaped with a scar-like mark; names Udumbeeswarar, Puttridam Kondar,
  Paarathazhumbar appear in sources. Second tradition: demon Magaran slain by Murugan, who came
  from Thirukazhukundram on the elephant Iravatham; a sculpture of Murugan on an elephant is
  linked to this. (Sources vary on details.)
- Architecture: Dravidian; five-tiered gopuram; present masonry of Vijayanagara/Nayak period
  (16th c.) over an older Chola (or per some blogs Pallava) shrine; inscriptions reported of
  Kulothunga Chola I, Sundara Pandya and Vijayanagara times.
- Festivals: Brahmotsavam in Masi (Feb–Mar) is the most prominent of four annual festivals;
  Maha Shivaratri, Navaratri, Masi Magam, Vaikasi Visakam special poojas; monthly Pradosham
  (generic). Worship for reunion of separated couples and relief from planetary affliction (Tamil source).
- Access: Kanchipuram–Uthiramerur highway via Orikkai; nearest rail town Kanchipuram, junction
  Chengalpattu; Chennai airport.
- Timings (conflicting): Wikipedia — six rituals 6:00 am–8:30 pm; a 2020 blog — 7:30–12:00 and 17:00–20:00.
- **Not verified:** the name "Adaikalam Katha Nathar" for this temple; which king founded it and
  the original century/dynasty (Chola vs Pallava; century 7 used for the Tevaram era);
  exact coordinates; current timings; bus service to the village; the "vinai-neekkum" verse
  content and verse count; the Murugan/Magaran story details; distance to the airport (not stated).
