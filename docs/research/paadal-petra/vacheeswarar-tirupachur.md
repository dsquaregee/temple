# Vacheeswarar (Pachuranathar) Temple, Tirupachur (vacheeswarar-tirupachur)

Region: Paadal Petra Sthalams — Thondai Nadu (Tiruvallur district). Tradition: Shaivism.

- Deity: Shiva as Vacheeswarar (also spelled Vaacheeswarar / Vaseeswarar; local name
  Pachuranathar, "lord of the bamboo grove"). Worshipped as a swayambhu (self-risen)
  linga. Consort named in sources as Thangaathali (Thankadali / Thankatali) Amman and,
  in Sanskritised form, Pasupati Nayaki; other listed names: Mohanambal, Panaimulai
  Nachiyar.
- Counted among the Paadal Petra Sthalams of the Thondai Nadu group; sung in the Tevaram.
  One source attributes the hymns to Tirunavukkarasar (Appar, two hymns) and Tirugnana
  Sambandar (one hymn) — placing its fame in the 7th century (used century 7).
- Sthala purana / name: the place was once a dense bamboo forest ("pachu" = bamboo),
  where the linga was found, giving the Lord the name Pachuranathar. The linga is said to
  bear a scar/mark from the time of its discovery (struck during clearing/excavation),
  for which the deity is traditionally worshipped without being touched.
- Legends: (1) Vishnu worshipped Shiva here to regain purity after defeating the demons
  Madhu and Kaitabha. (2) The goddess performed severe penance to win Shiva; on accepting
  her he called her "en kadhaliye" ("my beloved"), hence Thangaathali, the beloved one —
  her shrine is sought for love and married life. (3) Adi Shankara is credited with
  installing a Srichakra here; the fierce Swarna Kali is said to have been subdued by
  Nandi and enshrined here.
- Architecture: east-facing Dravidian village temple; swayambhu linga in the sanctum;
  goddess Thangaathali / Pasupati Nayaki in her own shrine; a gathering of eleven forms
  of Ganesha within the precinct; Srichakra (Adi Shankara tradition) and a Swarna Kali
  image. Temple puranams loosely estimate age at ~1500–2000 years; Chola-era inscriptions
  reported.
- Founding: local tradition credits the early Cholas, specifically Karikala Chola, with
  building the stone temple after the linga was found in the forest.
- Coordinates: **town-level ~13.100, 79.862** — no exact temple coordinate found in
  sources. Thirupachur is an adjacent revenue village to Kadambathur (Wikipedia gives
  Kadambathur 13°05′58″N 79°51′42″E ≈ 13.099, 79.862); used that as a town-level proxy.
  Refine against a mapping source when possible.
- Access: ~5 km west of Tiruvallur on the Tiruvallur–Tiruttani road, near the Kadambattur
  junction; ~60 km from Chennai. Kadambathur (Chennai suburban line) nearest railway;
  Chennai International Airport nearest airport.
- Festivals: Maha Shivaratri is the foremost festival reported; monthly Pradosham
  observed. Full annual festival calendar not firmly documented in sources — kept general.

**Not verified:** exact temple coordinate (used town-level Kadambathur proxy);
founding dynasty/century of the structural temple (local tradition names Karikala Chola;
used century 7 for the Tevaram/Appar–Sambandar era); the alternate Lord-name
"Pasupatheeswarar" given in the task was not directly confirmed in sources (sources give
the consort as Pasupati Nayaki and the Lord as Vacheeswarar / Pachuranathar — reflected
the Pachuranathar name in the content); current timings (sources differ: 7am–12 & 4–8pm
vs 8am–12:30 & 4–8pm); number of gopuram tiers / precise dimensions; which specific
Tevaram padigams. Note: a separate Tiruvallur-district temple, Vaseeswarar Temple at
Thirupasoor, is sometimes conflated with this one in web sources — details here are kept
to those consistently reported for Thirupachur / Vacheeswarar.
