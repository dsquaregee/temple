# Sreevallabha Temple, Thiruvalla (Thiruvalvazh) — Malai Nadu Divya Desam

- Presiding deity: Sreevallabha (Sreevallabhan), a form of Vishnu; Nammalvar and Thirumangai Alvar are the Alvars most often named as having sung the shrine.
- Coordinates: temple ~9.3737 N, 76.5624 E (Wikipedia 9°22'25"N 76°33'45"E; Kerala Tourism 9.3743, 76.5619). Used: 9.374, 76.562. (The town's own Wikipedia coordinates differ and describe the town.)
- History: the Thiruvalla (Huzur Treasury) copper plates, in Vattezhuthu with some Grantha, are temple records dated to the 10th–11th centuries; earliest Malayalam prose inscriptions from the temple dated to the 12th c. Sources disagree wildly on legendary age; not used.
- Architecture: Garuda sthamba (flag mast) of a single granite block, crowned with a stone Garuda, attributed in tradition to Perunthachan; murals on the sreekovil; Kathakali stage before the east entrance, Kathakali offered by devotees almost daily.
- Legend: a Brahmin woman's devotion; Vishnu as a brahmachari, and the demon Thukalasura slain (spelling varies). A separate Sudarshana shrine could not be confirmed and is not claimed.
- Unverified: detailed festival calendar (Vaikunta Ekadashi, annual utsavam stated generically).
