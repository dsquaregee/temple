# Thiruooragam — Ulagalantha Perumal / Trivikrama (Kanchipuram)

Thondai Nadu Divya Desam. Assigned id: `ulagalantha-thiruooragam`. Master list correct.
The Ulagalantha Perumal temple in Periya (Shiva) Kanchi houses FOUR Divya Desams in its
precincts: Thiruooragam (the main sanctum), Thiruneeragam, Thirukkaragam and Thirukkarvanam
(see `jagadeeshwara-thiruneeragam.md`, `karunakara-thirukkaragam.md`, `kalvar-thirukkarvanam.md`).

## Facts
- Presiding deity: Vishnu as Ulagalantha Perumal / Trivikrama (the Vamana avatara grown to
  measure the worlds); consort Amudhavalli (Amuthavalli) Thayar (Wikipedia). One travel site
  gives "Aranavalli" — not used.
- Thiruooragam is the Divya Desam of the main sanctum; the lord is also called Ooragathan, and
  tradition holds he also appeared in a smaller form as Adisesha (uragam = serpent); a shrine
  with the image of Adisesha is in the complex (temples-india blog; incredibleindia.gov.in).
- Legend: Vamana, the dwarf avatara, came to humble the asura king Mahabali; asked for three
  paces of land, grew as Trivikrama, and for the third step Mahabali offered his head
  (Wikipedia; incredibleindia). Tradition: the Lord showed himself as Adisesha (Ooragathan).
- Facing west (Wikipedia). Vimana: Srikara Vimanam. Tanks: Naga Tirtham, Shesha Tirtham (Wikipedia).
- History: believed built by the Pallavas, with later contributions by Medieval Cholas,
  Vijayanagara kings and Madurai Nayaks (Wikipedia). Inscriptions of Pallava, Chola and
  Sambuvarayar rulers; one source cites an inscription of 846 CE under Nandivarman III and one
  of c. 1110 CE under Kulothunga Chola I recording a royal visit and land gift
  (incredibleindia.gov.in) — phrased as "inscriptions from the Pallava and Chola periods".
- Administered by the HR&CE Department of Tamil Nadu (Wikipedia).
- The four shrines are believed to have once been independent temples; a single verse of
  Thirumangai Alvar names them together (incredibleindia.gov.in). Thirumangai Alvar's hymn
  confirmed; pasuram counts NOT confirmed this session (search budget exhausted). Thirumazhisai
  Alvar's Thiruchanda Viruttam verse naming Ooragam (standing), Padagam (seated) and Vehka
  (reclining) is well known in the tradition but was not re-confirmed by search — mentioned
  only cautiously in the entry.
- Image height (often quoted as ~35 ft) NOT confirmed — entry says only "towering".
- Festivals: not confirmed in detail; entry uses general Vaishnava observances (Vaikunta
  Ekadashi, annual Brahmotsavam, Margazhi recitation) phrased generally.
- Coordinates (Wikipedia infobox): 12°50′21″N 79°42′18″E → 12.8392, 79.7050.
- century: 8 (Alvar hymns).
