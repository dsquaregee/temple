## Identity
- One of the 108 Divya Desams (Vada Nadu) as Thiruvadari / Badarikashramam; also one of the Char Dham and of the Pancha Badri.
- Deity: Vishnu as Badrinarayan (Badri Narayanan), in a seated meditative posture *(posture general reference)*, image of black stone (Shaligram) *(general reference)*. Consort (Divya Desam lists): Aravindavalli.
- Alvars: Periyalvar and Thirumangai Alvar. Verse references: Thirumangai 968–987 (two decads, Periya Thirumozhi 1.3–1.4) and 2673 (Periya Thirumadal); Periyalvar 399. A quiz source gives "eleven" as a count — counts conflict, so no count is used in copy.
- Shrines in the complex include Lakshmi Narasimha, Adi Shankara, Nara-Narayana, Ghantakarna, Vedanta Desika and Ramanuja.

## Location
- On the Alaknanda in Chamoli district, Garhwal; elevation ~3,100 m.
- Coordinates: 30°44′41″N 79°29′28″E → 30.7447, 79.4912 (Wikipedia infobox, via search).
- Open about six months a year (roughly April/May to November) because of Himalayan winter.

## History
- Adi Shankara is credited (9th c. in some sources, 8th in others) with re-establishing the pilgrimage; tradition says he recovered the image from the Alaknanda (Narada Kund) and enshrined it near the Tapt Kund.
- Later care by Garhwal kings; rebuilt after earthquake damage (1803) *(unverified detail — not used)*.
- Chief priest (Rawal) traditionally a Namboodiri Brahmin from Kerala *(general reference, widely known; search did not confirm — used carefully as "by long custom")*.
- Century used: 8 (Alvar hymns).

## Architecture
- About 50 ft (official BKTC site) / sanctum roof ~15 m, with small cupola and gilt roof.
- Colourful main gate, the Singhdwar, reached by a staircase, with Garuda.
- Stone façade with arched windows; three parts — garbhagriha, darshan mandapa, sabha mandapa *(general reference)*.
- Tapt Kund: hot sulphur springs just below the temple; pilgrims bathe before darshan.

## Legends
- Vishnu performed penance here; Lakshmi sheltered him as the badari (jujube) tree — hence Badri / Badrinarayan; the deity is "worshipped under a jujube tree" (Divya Desam source).
- Nara and Narayana, the twin sages, did penance at Badari (the Nara and Narayana peaks flank the town).

## Festivals
- Opening and closing ceremonies of the season; Mata Murti ka Mela (Sept) and Badri-Kedar festival *(general reference, not confirmed by search)*. In winter worship moves down to Joshimath/Pandukeshwar *(general reference)*.

## Getting there
- Road from Rishikesh/Haridwar via Devprayag, Rudraprayag, Joshimath (~300 km); rail at Rishikesh/Haridwar; airport Jolly Grant (Dehradun).
