# Neelamegha (Sowriraja) Perumal Temple, Thirukannapuram

Divya Desam, Chola Nadu. Master list name/place confirmed. Wikipedia title: "Neelamegha Perumal Temple".

## Facts
- Deities: moolavar Neelamegha Perumal; utsavar Sowriraja Perumal; consort Kannapura Nayaki (Thirukannapura Nayagi, also called Padmini).
- Coordinates (Wikipedia): 10°52′06″N 79°42′15″E → 10.8683, 79.7042.
- Alvars: Thirumangai, Nammalvar, Kulasekara, Andal and Periyalvar (sources differ on counts: "128 total"; Thirumangai "45"; one says a century of verses — no count used). Periyalvar describes the shrine as enduring even through the deluge (pralaya).
- One of the Pancha Krishnaranya Kshetrams.
- History: believed built by the Cholas with later additions by the Thanjavur Nayaks. Sthala legend: a king built it with the help of Vishwakarma.
- Architecture: a granite wall encloses all shrines and three of its seven bodies of water; tanks Nitya Pushkarini (and Ananda Saras). Rajagopuram tier count conflicts (3 vs 7) — not stated. Sanctum faces east (per Thanjai Mamani Koil article).
- Sowri legend: the priest Ranga Bhattar, poor, reused a garland; the visiting king found a hair in it; the priest said it was the Lord's hair; the Lord appeared with thick tresses (sowri = hair). The utsavar is adorned with hair in Brahmotsavam processions.
- Vibhishana: on every Amavasya the Lord is brought out to give darshan to Vibhishana, recalling his wish to see the Lord's gait/standing form (Nadai Azhagu Sevai).
- Festivals: Brahmotsavam with Sowri adornment; chariot festival in Vaikasi; Masi Magam in Masi; Amavasya Vibhishana sevai. Pongal offered at the last puja of the day.

## Not verified
- Masi Magam sea-bath location; "Munaiyadhariyan" pongal legend; vimana name; exact Alvar counts.
