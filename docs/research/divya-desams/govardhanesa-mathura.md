# Thiru Vadamathurai — Govardhanesan (Mathura, Uttar Pradesh)

Vada Nadu Divya Desam. Assigned id: `govardhanesa-mathura`.

**Identification (read first).** The temple the Alvars sang at Vadamathurai does not
survive as an identifiable structure. Sri Vaishnava references name the Divya Desam's lord
Govardhanesan (Govardhana Nesan) / Balakrishnan and treat Mathura, Vrindavan and
Govardhan together as one Divya Desam. Pilgrim guides associate the shrine with Krishna's
birthplace at Mathura — the Sri Krishna Janmasthan (Keshavdev temple) complex — and one
tour-operator source calls the Govardhana Nesa Perumal temple "also Krishna Janmabhoomi".
One Sri Vaishnava archive message is summarised (in search text only) as naming a
"Rangamandir" as the main temple — plausibly the Sri Ranganatha (Rangji) temple of
Vrindavan, but this could not be confirmed. The catalog entry therefore presents the Divya
Desam as Mathura itself, honoured today chiefly at the Janmasthan, and says plainly that the
Alvars' temple is lost. Coordinates are those of the Krishna Janmasthan complex.
No disputes or politics are mentioned in the content (per brief).

## Facts
- Moolavar: Govardhanesan, also called Balakrishnan; standing, facing east (srivaishnavan.com).
- Thayar: Satyabhama Nachiyar.
- Vimanam: Govardhana Vimanam (srivaishnavan.com; ibiblio Sripedia).
- Theertham: Yamuna (all sources); one listing also names Indra Theertham and Govardhana Theertham.
- Mangalasasanam: Periyalvar, Andal, Thondaradippodi Alvar, Thirumangai Alvar, Nammalvar.
  Pasuram count conflicts (one source: 50 total — Periyalvar 16, Nammalvar 10, Andal 19,
  Thondaradippodi 1, Thirumangai 4; another gives far lower Andal counts) — count NOT used.
- Andal's Thiruppavai verse 5 opens "Mayanai mannu Vadamadurai maindanai" — the best-known
  reference to Vadamathurai in the Prabandham (well known; text not re-fetched).
- Mathura is one of the seven Mukti Kshetras (Sapta Puri).
- Sthala purana: Shatrughna, at Rama's bidding, slew the demon Lavanasura and founded
  Mathura on the Yamuna; Krishna's birth here; the name Govardhanesan recalls Krishna lifting
  Govardhan hill to shelter the cowherds from Indra's storm (Govardhan ~ 8 miles / c. 20 km away).
- Krishna Janmasthan complex (Wikipedia): coordinates 27°30′17″N 77°40′11″E (27.505, 77.670);
  three main structures — Keshavdev temple, the Garbh Griha (the birthplace shrine), and
  Bhagavata Bhavan (Radha-Krishna); modern temple consecrated 1958, built with support of
  industrialist families (Dalmia, Birla; Hanuman Prasad Poddar), Sri Krishna Janmasthan Trust.
  Site religiously significant since at least the 6th c. BCE (per Wikipedia summary).
- Festivals: Krishna Janmashtami (main), Vaikunta Ekadashi among Sri Vaishnavas; Holi season
  in Braj (general knowledge, phrased generally).
- century: 8 used (Alvar hymns) — earlier temple dates in secondary sources not firm.
