## Identity
- The only Divya Desam outside India; Sri Vaishnava name Thiru Salagramam / Sri Saligramam. Region: Vada Nadu.
- Deity: Vishnu as Muktinath ("lord of liberation"); in Divya Desam lists the moolavar is Sri Moorthy. Vaishnava tradition associates the shrine with Sridevi and Bhudevi; one account names Sridevi, Bhudevi, Niladevi and "Gotadevi".
- Alvars: Thirumangai Alvar — a decad of 10 pasurams, Periya Thirumozhi 1.5 (tradition says he sang from the nearest place he could reach, not the shrine itself). Periyalvar names the lord "Salagramamudaiya Nambi". Kulasekhara Alvar is also credited with a reference in one account *(not confirmed)*.
- Revered by Buddhists as Chumig Gyatsa, "Hundred Waters"; Tibetan Buddhists associate the site with Avalokiteshvara and with Guru Rinpoche (Padmasambhava). Damodar Kunda (Tibetan "Men-Chu") is linked with Guru Rinpoche.

## Location
- Altitude about 3,710 m, at the foot of the Thorong La pass in Mustang district; near the village of Ranipauwa (sometimes itself called Muktinath).
- Coordinates used: 28.8167, 83.8714 *(unverified — Wikipedia infobox from reference memory; search could not confirm)*.

## History
- Present pagoda-style temple credited to Queen Subarna Prabha (Nepal), c. 1815 (muktinath.org gives 1815 for the Vishnu temple and the Chenrezig temple). The image and the site are older; Puranic texts already link Salagrama to the Gandaki.
- Earliest firm attestation: Alvar hymns (c. 8th c.) → century 8.

## Architecture
- Small pagoda-style shrine with a gilded/golden roof.
- 108 water spouts (Muktidhara) in a semicircular wall about seven feet high, spouts carved as bull's/cow's heads (sources vary).
- Two ponds in front: Mukti Kunda; one named Saraswati Kunda.
- Nearby Jwala Mai shrine with natural flame over water *(unverified, general reference)* — not used in copy.

## Legends
- Salagrama (ammonite fossil) stones from the Kali Gandaki are worshipped as Vishnu; Damodar Kunda is regarded as a source of the Shaligram and the Gandaki.
- Vrinda/Tulasi legend: Vrinda, wife of Jalandhara, cursed Vishnu to become stone; Vishnu is worshipped as the Shaligram, and the Tulasi leaf is inseparable from Shaligram worship. Versions differ; one holds Muktinath as the place Vishnu was freed of the curse.

## Festivals / seasons
- No firm festival calendar confirmed. Pilgrimage concentrated in the open seasons (spring/autumn); bathing under the 108 spouts and in the kundas is the central rite. Shared by Hindu and Buddhist pilgrims.

## Getting there
- Pokhara → Jomsom (flight or road via Beni), then road to Ranipauwa and a short climb to the temple. Altitude acclimatisation advised.
