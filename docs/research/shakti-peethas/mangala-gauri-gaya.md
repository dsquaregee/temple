# Mangala Gauri Temple, Gaya (mangala-gauri-gaya)

Region: Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha — Gaya district, Bihar. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: **Mangala Gauri** ("Gayayam Mangala Gauri" in the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha stotram; stotram traditionally attributed to Adi Shankara — attribution not independently confirmed). Some sources name the Gaya goddess Sarvamangala instead; naming varies across lists. One source (Wanderlog) classes the temple as an Upa-Shakti Peeth; Bihar Tourism, Incredible India and the stotram listing treat it as one of the eighteen Maha Shakti Peethas.
- Bhairava: **not verified.** No source found names the Bhairava (a visitor review mentions a Kalabhairava statue under a tree; not sufficient). The entry does not name one.
- Sati body part: **breast (stana)** fell here (several sources; one specifies the right breast, another "two round stones representing the breasts of Sati" in the sanctum). The breast is read as the sign of nourishment (Bihar Tourism). Vishnu's chakra / dismemberment retelling is the standard account.
- Coordinates: **~24.775, 85.002** (approximate hill/temple location from a search-result summary; not cross-checked against a map). Treat as hill-level.
- Setting: temple atop Mangalagauri hill (also called **Bhasmakut**), overlooking the Falgu (Phalgu) river; reached by steps and a motorable road; faces east.
- Defining legend: the Sati / Daksha story with the breast falling at Gaya. Gaya context from Wikipedia "Phalgu River": Sita cursed the Phalgu to have no water at Gaya and blessed the Akshayavat so that pindadaan is performed at the tree (used only as a brief setting sentence).
- Pind-daan link: Gaya is the principal place for shraddha/pind-daan; Pitru Paksha crowds (2.49 million pilgrims reported in one year). **No source confirmed that pind-daan is performed at Mangala Gauri itself or the ritual order relative to Vishnupad**; the entry says only that pilgrims to Gaya often also climb the hill.
- Scriptures: said to be mentioned in the Padma, Vayu, Agni, Devi Bhagavata and Markandeya Puranas (aggregator sources; passages not located). No source linked it to the Gaya Mahatmya.
- History/dating: present temple "15th century" (Bihar Tourism and others); one source gives **1459 CE**; Wikipedia's infobox gives 1300 CE and names Maadho Giri Ji Maharaj (Baba Dandi Swami) as creator, which conflicts with its own text. Entry uses century 15 and says builders are not firmly recorded.
- Architecture: small mandapa before the sanctum; sanctum holds a symbol of the Goddess (aniconic), described as two round stones; "finely carved ancient relief sculptures"; compound has shrines of Shiva, Durga, Dakshina Kali, Mahishasuramardini, Ganesha, Hanuman. One listing says "Cave style" (likely wrong; not used).
- Festivals: Navratri (Chaitra Mar–Apr); Mangala Gauri vrat on Tuesdays of Shravana (women fast; offerings of 16 bangles, seven fruits, five sweets per one source; 2026 Purnimanta dates 4, 11, 18, 25 Aug per one source); Pitru Paksha at Gaya (Sep).
- Access: Gaya Junction (railway); Gaya International Airport (Bodh Gaya); temple timings ~6:00–20:00 daily (two sources).

**Not verified:** Bhairava; exact coordinates; builder and exact date of the present temple; the specific Puranic passages; whether pind-daan is offered at this shrine; which breast fell and the nature of the sanctum stones (single sources); dress code and photography rules (stated generally); transport specifics; Gaya Mahatmya connection; classification as Maha vs Upa peetha (sources differ).
