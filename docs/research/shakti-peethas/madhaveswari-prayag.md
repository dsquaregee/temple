# Madhaveswari Shakti Peetha (Alopi Devi Temple), Prayagraj (madhaveswari-prayag)

Region: Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Prayage Madhaveswari") — Prayagraj (Allahabad) district, Uttar Pradesh. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: **Madhaveswari** in the Ashtadasha stotram; the Peetha Nirnaya Tantra names the
  Prayaga Devi as **Lalita**, Bhairava **Bhava** (greenmesg.org Prayaga entry). In popular
  worship the goddess is Alopi Devi / Alop Shankari (also listed as Lalita Temple or
  Mahadeveshwari Temple by bhaktibharat.com).
- Sati body-part tradition: **fingers** fell at Prayaga (Peetha Nirnaya; one stotram-based list
  also gives "fingers"). At the Alopi temple the local variant is that the hand/finger (or
  claw/nail of the hand) of Sati fell into a pond and vanished (alop = disappeared), so no idol
  is kept. The Ashtadasha stotram itself names the goddess and place, not the body part.
- **Identity of the shrine is unsettled (presented honestly in the entry).** Candidates:
  (1) **Alopi Devi Mandir, Alopibagh** — the popular identification (hindupad lists "Prayaga
  Madhaveswari" as the 14th of 18 and says the goddess is "also popular as Alopi Mata / Maa
  Lalita"); (2) **Lalita Devi Temple, Meerapur** on the Yamuna bank (~5 km from the Sangam) —
  Wikipedia's Alopi article and YatraDham say it is the *only* Shakti Peetha in Prayagraj and
  call the Alopi claim "debatable"; (3) some lists also add Kalyani Devi. Name chosen for the
  entry: "Madhaveswari Shakti Peetha (Alopi Devi Temple)" with the locale/coords at Alopibagh.
- Coordinates: **25.4442, 81.8709** — single source (bhaktibharat.com listing 25.4441531 N,
  81.8708747 E). English Wikipedia gives locality only (Alopibagh, 211006). Treat as
  temple-level but unconfirmed; refine against a mapping source.
- Defining legend/worship: no idol; a wooden palanquin (**doli**) hangs/rests over a small
  pond/kund of water (bhaktibharat: "cradle hanging on a ten feet wide pond") and is worshipped.
  Oral-history version (Wikipedia, Tripadvisor review): a bride's marriage procession was
  attacked by dacoits in a forest and the bride vanished from the palanquin; the empty doli
  is worshipped. Devotees tie a raksha sutra while making wishes (bhaktibharat).
- Prayag context: Triveni Sangam (Ganga–Yamuna–invisible Sarasvati); Akshayavat; Beni Madhava
  and Bindu Madhava among the Dwadasha Madhava Vishnu forms (the "Madhava" link to the name
  Madhaveswari is **not** sourced, so the entry does not interpret the name).
- History/patronage: no dated founding record found. Wikipedia (Alopi Devi Mandir) says Maratha
  warrior Mahadji Shinde developed the Sangam area during his stay in 1771–72, and Maharani
  Baizabai Scindia renovated the Sangam ghats and temples in the 1800s — general Prayag patronage,
  not temple-specific. **century 18 is a placeholder** anchored to that patronage; the tradition
  is far older.
- Architecture: very little sourced. Wikipedia and bhaktibharat describe the doli above a pond
  with no statue; no verified detail on the shikhara, mandapa or plan. "Nagara/North Indian"
  style is a general descriptor, not sourced to this temple.
- Festivals: Navratri (grand fair, nine forms worshipped; bhaktibharat), Dussehra; Magh Mela and
  Kumbh/Maha Kumbh bring citywide crowds (travel guide). (Spring/autumn Navratri cycle is
  general knowledge.)
- Access: Alopibagh, Prayagraj 211006; ~3.5 km from Prayagraj Junction (bhaktibharat; other
  guides say ~5 km from railway/city centre); distance to the Sangam conflicts across sources
  (from "very close" to 8–9 km by road) so the entry says "a few kilometres"; Prayagraj has an
  airport (distance not sourced).
- **Not verified:** which shrine is the true Ashtadasha "Madhaveswari" peetha; founding date,
  patron and century of the Alopi temple; temple-level coordinates (single source); architecture
  (shikhara/mandapa/pool dimensions); current timings (sources: 6 am–10 pm; 5 am–9 pm); the
  "Monday and Friday Mata ka Mela" mentioned by one blog (not repeated); photography and dress
  rules (no temple-specific source; text is general and hedged); airport distance; Bhairava
  "Bhava" at Prayaga appears in the Peetha Nirnaya verse as quoted by greenmesg.org only.
