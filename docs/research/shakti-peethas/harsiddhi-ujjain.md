# Harsiddhi Mata Temple, Ujjain (harsiddhi-ujjain)

Region: Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Ujjayinyam Mahakali" in Adi Shankara's Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha stotram) — Ujjain district, Madhya Pradesh. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: **Harsiddhi**, worshipped as **Mahakali** (the stotram's name for the Ujjain seat) and as **Mangalachandika / Mangal-Chandi** (the name used in the Sati-peetha lists and the "ancient" family-goddess tradition of Vikramaditya). Some sources equate her with Vaishnavi/Durga. Distinct from the Mahakaleshwar Jyotirlinga (Shiva) a short walk away.
- Bhairava: **Lambakarna** (Lambkarneshwar) per a Wikipedia-derived peetha list (Avanti Devi / Lambakarna). Another list pairs the Mangala Chandika elbow-peetha with "Kapilambara" — only one source, not corroborated.
- Sati body-part tradition: **elbow (kurpara)** is the tradition most often cited for the Harsiddhi / Mangala Chandika shrine (ujjain.nic.in, MP Tourism, Srimandir, Wikipedia-derived lists; "13th of the 51 Shakti Peethas"). **Sources conflict:** other peetha lists place the **upper lip (oshtha)** at Avanti/Ujjain (Bhairava Parvata on the Shipra). The app text follows the elbow tradition and notes that a listing differs.
- Stotram: Adi Shankara's Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha stotram (attributed to the 8th century; hindupedia, hinduwebsite) opens the Ujjain verse with "Ujjayinyam Mahakali". 
- Coordinates: **approximate 23.182, 75.767** — beside Rudra Sagar, a short walk west of the Mahakaleshwar temple (Ujjain city centre is ~23.183, 75.777). No sourced temple-level coordinate found in this session; refine against a map source.
- Defining legend: King **Vikramaditya** (legendary ruler of Ujjain) took Harsiddhi as his family goddess and, in the tradition, offered his head to her; she restored it each time, until the last offering when it was not restored, ending his rule. **Sources disagree on the count** (eleven offerings in several sources; a twelve-yearly offering in others), so the app text gives no number. Also: Harsiddhi is said to have been invited by Vikramaditya to his kingdom of Ujjain and worshipped daily by him (apnayatra / hindutone).
- Architecture: Maratha-influenced temple (Incredible India; Wikipedia "Ancient monuments in Ujjain": reconstructed during the Maratha period). Four entrances, main one east. Two tall **deepmala / deep-stambh lamp pillars** in front, regarded as Maratha work and traditionally credited to Vikramaditya, studded with lamp-holders lit at evening aarti and in Navratri. **Lamp counts and heights conflict** (726 holders; 1,011 diyas combined; 15 ft / 51 ft / 84 ft) — the app text gives no numbers. Sanctum images: Annapurna, Mahalakshmi and Mahasaraswati stacked (Srimandir / MP Tourism); Sri Yantra shrine in the complex; carved assembly-hall pillars described by one source as Paramara-period. A Wikipedia list credits Ranoji Scindia with renovation (single source).
- Festivals: Navratri (Chaitra, March–April, and Ashwin, September–October), when the lamp pillars are lit; daily morning (~7–8 am) and evening (~6–7 pm) aarti with lighting of the deep-stambhs.
- Access: Ujjain (Madhya Pradesh); temple beside Rudra Sagar, a short walk from Mahakaleshwar, and on the usual Mahakaleshwar–Harsiddhi–Ram Ghat route. Ujjain Junction is the railhead; Indore airport is the nearest air access (~55 km, general knowledge). Timings reported variously: darshan ~5 am–7 pm (one listing) to 9–10 pm (others); extended in Navratri.
- **Not verified:** founding date and builder (legend says Vikramaditya; present structure Maratha-era — century 18 used; Scindia attribution single-source; Paramara-period pillars single-source); the exact lamp-pillar height and number of lamps; the Sati body part (elbow vs upper lip); Bhairava name (Lambakarna vs Kapilambara); temple-level coordinate; Indore airport distance; current timings; photography rules inside the sanctum.
- Do not conflate with: Harsiddhi Mata temple at Rajpipla/Kotheshwar, Gujarat (a separate shrine claiming the goddess came from Ujjain), Harsiddhi Devi Mandir, Rangir (Sagar district), or Harsiddhi temple, Indore.
