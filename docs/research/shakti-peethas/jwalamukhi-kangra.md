# Jwalamukhi Temple, Kangra (jwalamukhi-kangra)

Region: Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha — Jwalamukhi (Jawalamukhi), Kangra district, Himachal Pradesh. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: Jwalamukhi / Jwala Devi, **Vaishnavi** in the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha stotram ("Jwalayam Vaishnavi"; stotram traditionally attributed to Adi Shankara — attribution not independently confirmed). No idol: worshipped as natural flames (usually nine) rising from rock fissures.
- Bhairava: **Unmatta Bhairava** (per Wikipedia and the Kangra district sources).
- Sati body part: **tongue** (jihva) fell here. One variant account has Vishnu cutting the body with his chakra; the tongue tradition is consistent.
- Coordinates: **31°52′31″N, 76°19′27″E = 31.8753, 76.3242** (Wikipedia infobox, temple-level). Not the Khrew (Kashmir) Jwala Ji, which is a different temple.
- Nine flames (HP Kangra official list): Mahakali, Annapurna, Chandi, Hinglaj, Vindhyavasini (Vidhya Basni), Mahalakshmi, Saraswati, Ambika, Anji Devi. Spellings vary across sources. Central flame from a hollowed rock is regarded as the mouth of Mahakali/the Goddess.
- Defining legend: the eternal flame and the Akbar story. Akbar is said to have tried to extinguish the flames (iron disc, water channel), then offered a golden umbrella (chhatra) which turned into an unknown metal (Wikipedia "Jwala (goddess)"; variant: it turned to copper; kangratemples.hp.gov.in pairs it with the Dhyanu Bhagat story). A Jawala Ji guide notes it is oral tradition with no mention in Akbarnama/Mughal records; the umbrella shown today is brass/copper. The entry presents it as local tradition, not history.
- Gorakh Dibbi (Gorakh Tibbi): Nath shrine just above the temple; legend of Gorakhnath leaving a pot (khichri) to boil and promising to return; the "boiling" water is gas bubbling (visitor account). Exact wording of the legend varies and is only partly corroborated.
- History: Local tradition credits Raja Bhumi Chand Katoch of Kangra (Durga devotee, guided by a dream). Earliest textual mention cited: the 14th-century Tarikh-i-Firuz-Shahi (Firuz Shah Tughlaq's Kangra campaign). Alexander Cunningham identifies a c. 650 CE Chinese envoy's account of hot and cold water from rocks with Jwalamukhi (not used in the entry). Sikh-era patronage: gilding of the dome attributed to Maharaja Ranjit Singh (visit cited 1815); silver doors attributed variously to Kharak Singh or Sher Singh. The entry says only "later rulers, among them the Sikh court at Lahore"; century set to 14 (earliest documented mention).
- Architecture: modest hall with gilded dome and several pinnacles, silver-plated entrance doors, small mandapa in front (HP / Wikipedia descriptions). Described variously as "modern Muslim style" or "modern design"; entry uses "North Indian Himalayan".
- Festivals: Navratri fairs; Wikipedia states a fair in July/August during Navratras (unusual; not used). Entry uses Chaitra (Mar–Apr) and Ashwin (Sep–Oct) Navratri from general knowledge. Aartis per HP Kangra site: Mangal, Bhog, Evening and Shayan; official portal lists two main aartis (7:00 am and 7:00 pm winter; 6:00 am and 8:00 pm summer).
- Access: ~51–56 km from Dharamshala by road; Gaggal (Kangra) airport ~46–52 km; Pathankot ~110–123 km (nearest broad-gauge); Jwalamukhi Road station on the Kangra Valley narrow-gauge ~20 km (single source).
- Timings: official Kangra temples portal — winter 7:00–11:00 and 12:30–21:30; summer 6:00–11:30 and 12:30–22:00; other sites give 5:00–22:00. Entry gives only a general pattern.

**Not verified:** Bhumi Chand's date and the founding story beyond local tradition; the Ranjit Singh gilding date (one source gives 1815, another an implausible 1765); which Sikh ruler gave the silver doors; Sansar Chand attribution (Chamba vs Kangra, one source); the exact Gorakhnath legend; Pandava association (not found in any source); festival dates (Chaitra/Ashwin Navratri stated from general knowledge); photography rules inside the sanctum (stated generally); dress code details; whether the 1905 earthquake damaged the shrine (single weak source, not used).
