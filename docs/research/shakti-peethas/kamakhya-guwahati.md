# Kamakhya Temple, Guwahati (kamakhya-guwahati)

Region: Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Kamarupe Kamakhya") — Kamrup Metropolitan district, Assam. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: **Kamakhya** (Kamakhya Devi, "Mother of desire/creation"). Bhairava: **Umananda**
  (Peetha Nirnaya Tantra, via greenmesg.org's Kamagiri entry; Umananda's shrine is on
  Peacock Island / Bhasmachal in the Brahmaputra, brick temple built 1694 under Ahom king
  Gadadhar Singha per Wikipedia's Umananda article).
- Sati body-part tradition: the **yoni** of Sati fell on Nilachal Hill (Kamagiri). The Kalika
  Purana says the yoni-mandala turned to stone and the Devi abides in it. Note: the
  Ashtadasha stotram itself names the goddess/place (Kamarupe Kamakhya) but gives no body
  part; the body-part attribution is from the wider Pitha lists (Peetha Nirnaya, 51-peetha
  tradition). Counted among the most sacred peethas (with Gaya and Ujjain in one tradition,
  as symbolising creation).
- Coordinates: **26.1664, 91.7055** (English Wikipedia infobox 26.166426 N, 91.705509 E).
  The "Kamakhya, Guwahati" locality article gives 26.16639, 91.70639; an Assamese Wikipedia
  draft gives 91.7868 E, which looks erroneous and was not used.
- Defining legend: Sati's self-immolation at Daksha's yajna; Shiva carries her body; Vishnu's
  chakra scatters it; the yoni falls on Nilachal. Kalika Purana: Kamadeva, burnt by Shiva,
  regains his form here by the goddess's grace, hence "Kamarupa". Sanctum has no image — a
  yoni-shaped rock cleft fed by a perennial underground spring.
- History: Kalika Purana (dated by one source to c. 9th c.; Pala-era) and Yogini Tantra make
  Kamakhya a Tantric centre (kamakhya.org says since 8th c.). The temple was destroyed — sources
  disagree (Husain Shah c. 1498 vs. Kalapahar 1553, the latter "assumed" by some historians
  with little material evidence). Rebuilt: Biswa Singha (founder of the Koch dynasty) began
  rebuilding early 1500s; son **Nara Narayana completed it in 1565**, brother Chilarai
  supervising; architect Meghamukdam named by kamakhya.org; present temple reuses ruins of
  an 11th–12th-century stone temple (one source). Ahom kings (17th–19th c.) expanded the
  complex; Wikipedia cites inscriptions of Rajeswar Singha (1759) and Gaurinath Singha (1782).
  One account says Rudra Singha (r. 1696–1714) invited a Shakta mahant, Krishnaram
  Bhattacharyya, to care for the temple. Used century 16 (1565 rebuild).
- Architecture: **Nilachal style** — hybrid of Nagara with a hemispherical/polygonal
  beehive-like dome (brick over stone lower portion) on a cruciform plan; subterranean
  garbhagriha (no image, yoni-cleft with spring); antarala, calanta/Jagamohana, bhogmandir,
  natmandir (apsidal shell roof, Ahom period); small chala-style angashikhara shrines. Sources
  vary on the number of mandapas (Panchratna and Natyamandapa possibly 18th c.). Ten Mahavidya
  shrines on the hill, many rebuilt/renovated in Ahom era.
- Festivals: **Ambubachi Mela** (mid-June, four days; doors closed three days, reopening after
  Nivriti ritual; devotees receive Angodak and Angabastra); Durga Puja/Navaratri as the
  fortnight "Pakhua Puja" (Krishna Navami to Shukla Navami of Ashwina); **Manasa Puja**
  (three days from Shravana Sankranti, in the Natmandir) with Deodhani dance; Pohan Biya
  (Devi's marriage day); Basanti Puja.
- Access: ~8 km from Guwahati railway station (kamakhya.org; other pages 7–8.3 km); Lokpriya
  Gopinath Bordoloi Intl Airport ~20 km; Kamakhya railway station also in the city (distance
  sources conflict, omitted); road to the temple plus two staircases from the hill foot.
- **Not verified:** which invasion/date destroyed the earlier temple; identity/dating of
  the pre-1565 patrons (Mlechchha / Pala / earlier); exact mandapa count and the dating of
  individual halls; current timings (sources give 5:30 am open vs 7:30 am; closing about
  1 pm / reopen 2:30 pm to 5:30 pm) and paid fast-track ticket prices (Rs 500 / 501 /
  2000 reported); **photography/phone rules** (no Kamakhya-specific source found — text is
  general and hedged); the custom of offering red hibiscus and red cloth (general devotional
  practice, no source checked); exact Pohan Biya and Basanti Puja dates; whether Umananda
  is "Bhairava" in every list (Peetha Nirnaya says so).
