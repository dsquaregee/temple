# Shrinkhala Devi Shakti Peetha, Pandua (shrinkhala-devi-pandua)

Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Pradyumne Shrinkhala Devi" in the stotram ascribed to Adi Shankara). Pandua, Hooghly
district, West Bengal, India. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: Shrinkhala (Srinkhala / Srunkhala) Devi; shrinkhala = chain. Sometimes described as a form of Durga.
- "Pradyumna" in the stotram is the **place name** (Pradyumna / Pradyumnanagar), not the Bhairava; the task brief's
  "(Pradyumna)" was therefore treated as the place.
- Bhairava: **not found** in any source consulted; content says it is not firmly recorded.
- Sati body-part tradition: **stomach** (Wikipedia "Shakta pithas" table: "Shrinkala Temple, Pradmunyee (Pandua), West
  Bengal, Bhavatarini Pitham, part of stomach"; behindeverytemple.org agrees). One list table places Pradyumna in
  Gujarat - the location is contested between lists.
- Coordinates: **23.078, 88.279** - town-level for Pandua, Hooghly (Wikipedia 23.08N 88.28E; biodiversity record
  23.0780652, 88.2788636). Not a shrine coordinate: no shrine location is established. The Pandua Minar is within the town.
- Defining legend: Sati/Daksha yajna and the division of her body (stomach fell here). Sage Rishyasringa circled the hill
  at Pandua, felt the Goddess, and at her direction went to Sringeri, Karnataka (behindeverytemple.org, astroved.com;
  another version says he brought the Goddess to Sringeri).
- Present site / architecture: no temple standing. Pandua Minar, ~38 m (125 ft), brick Indo-Islamic tower, and the Bari
  Masjid, ASI-protected. Minar origin conflicts across sources: 13th-century victory tower (Shah Safiuddin / Sufiuddin
  against Pandu Raja, per travel accounts) vs built 1477 by Shamsuddin Yusuf Shah, with the claim that the minar and
  mosque replaced/destroyed the Shrinkhala Devi temple (one devotional source). The area is flat alluvial plain
  (Hooghly-Damodar plain, Wikipedia).
- Festivals: no Shrinkhala temple festival verified. A roughly month-long "Mela" in February in the minar grounds
  attended by Hindu and Muslim communities (~1 lakh visitors) is reported by behindeverytemple.org / astroved.com (the
  site names it "Mela Taala"; name not independently confirmed and its link to the Goddess is not established).
  Navaratri/Durga Puja stated only as the general Shakta season.
- Access: Pandua (station spelled Pundooah) on the Howrah-Bardhaman main line; ~60 km from Howrah (rome2rio 61 km
  straight, 65 km road); Hooghly district site gives Kolkata ~50 km SE; toto from the station to the minar.
  Beware the separate Pandua in Malda district (medieval Bengal capital) and Pandu Rajar Dhibi (Purba Bardhaman).
- Century: **8** used as the earliest textual attestation (the stotram, ascribed to Adi Shankara; attribution itself is
  traditional). No founding date for any temple is sourced.

## Not verified
- That the Hooghly Pandua is *the* Pradyumna of the stotram (disputed; Malda Pandua and a Gujarat location appear in other
  lists); the Bhairava name; whether any temple ever stood at the minar site; the claim that the temple was destroyed
  to build the minar/mosque (single source); the minar's date (13th c. vs 1477); the claim that Hindus have no
  access to the site (astroved.com - the content avoids stating it, saying only that the site is a protected monument
  shared with a mosque); the existence of a "hill" at Pandua (legend only; terrain is flat plain); the February fair's
  name and its link to the Goddess; current site timings and photography rules (assumed general ASI practice,
  not sourced).
