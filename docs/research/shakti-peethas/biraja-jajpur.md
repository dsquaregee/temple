# Biraja Temple, Jajpur (biraja-jajpur)

Region: Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Oddyane Girija Devi" in Adi Shankara's Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha stotram) — Jajpur district, Odisha (Biraja Kshetra, on the Vaitarani). Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess: **Biraja / Viraja**, identified in the stotram as **Girija**; in the Peetha Nirnaya / Tantra Chudamani lists the goddess of the Viraja Kshetra peetha is named **Vimala**. Worshipped as a two-armed **Mahishamardini** (a form of Durga / Chandi). Wikipedia: Brahmayamala Tantra (c. 7th century) already names Viraja as goddess of Utkala.
- Bhairava: **Jagannatha** per the Peetha Nirnaya verse cited at greenmesg.org (single source; not corroborated). A "Varaha" Bhairava was searched and not found.
- Sati body-part tradition: **navel (nabhi)** fell at Viraja Kshetra in **Utkala** (Tantra Chudamani, Wikipedia "Biraja Temple"). Wikipedia places the Oddiyana Peetha near the Vaitarani; the stotram's "Oddyane" is commonly identified with Jajpur but the Odyana location is debated (not settled in sources).
- Coordinates: **20.8338, 86.3381** (Wikipedia: 20°50′1.55″N 86°20′17.32″E; agrees with a second map-based source). Jajpur town ~20.85, 86.33 (20°51′N 86°20′E).
- Defining legend / identity: the two-armed Mahishamardini — one hand drives a spear into the chest of the buffalo-demon Mahishasura, the other grips his tail; one foot on the lion, one on Mahishasura. Her crown bears a small Ganesha, a crescent moon and a linga (Wikipedia and a pilgrimage guide). Local Puranic tradition (single source, hindupad / livehistoryindia type): Brahma performed a yajna on the Vaitarani and the goddess emerged from the fire as Biraja, giving the "Biraja Tirtha". Separate tradition: **Nabhi Gaya** well in the compound (navel of the Vaishnava demon Gayasura; shraddha rites for ancestors; Jajpur counted among Gaya kshetras), distinct from the Sati-navel tradition.
- Architecture: Kalinga (Odishan) style. East entrance through the **Simhadwara** flanked by lions astride elephants; black-stone **Simha Stambha** (lion pedestal) before the shrine; **rekha deula** over the sanctum (one source: squarish rekha deula with amalaka, kalasha and flag) and a **pidha deula** over the jagamohana; subsidiary shrines in the compound (incl. a Bagalamukhi shrine per one review). Compound wall ~250 sq m per one source (unclear). Height of the vimana not found.
- Date: Wikipedia — present temple built in the **11th century** (used: century 11; Somavamsi era); many tourism pages say 13th century (Jajati Keshari); one source credits Somavamsi king Yayati II (10th–11th c.); a pilgrim site claims a 4th-century (Gupta) image — uncorroborated. Jajpur was a Kesari/Somavamsi capital before Cuttack (Wikipedia, Varahanatha Temple).
- Festivals: **Sharadiya Durga Puja** — 16-day (Shodasha Dinatmaka) puja starting the Krishna Paksha Ashtami night before Mahalaya, ending Ashwin Shukla Navami; Aparajita Puja during Navratri; **Simhadhwaja ratha** nine-day chariot festival (documented as the only chariot procession for a goddess in Odisha — single-source phrasing); other observances: Prathamastami, Pana Sankranti, Raja Parva, Navanna, Savitri Amavasya; Varuni bath in the Vaitarani in Chaitra (one source). The Bali Dana rite between Ashtami and Navami is reported; **not included in the app text**.
- Access: Jajpur Town, on the Vaitarani; ~125 km north of Bhubaneswar (Wikipedia). Nearest airport Bhubaneswar (Biju Patnaik), railhead Jajpur Keonjhar Road (general knowledge — not verified in sources). Timings reported variously: morning ~5:30/6 am–1/1:30 pm and evening ~3–9/10 pm; confirm locally. Dress: modest, knees and shoulders covered; footwear off.
- **Not verified:** the exact founding date and patron (11th vs 13th century; Kesari vs Somavamsi); Bhairava name (Jagannatha — single source); Brahma-yajna origin tale (single source); vimana height; whether photography is permitted in outer courtyards (generic temple rule used); nearest railway station name and Bhubaneswar-airport distance; current timings; exact festival dates.
