# Shankari Devi Temple, Trincomalee (shankari-devi-trincomalee)

Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha (no. 1 in the stotram: "Lankayam Shankari Devi"). Trincomalee, Eastern Province,
Sri Lanka. Tradition: Shaktism. Distinct from the Shiva temple `koneswarar-trincomalee` (same headland, Swami Rock).

- Goddess: Shankari Devi (also remembered as Indrakshi in the Lanka Peetha verse of the Pitha Nirnaya).
- Bhairava: the Pitha Nirnaya verse for the Lanka peetha names **Indrakshi / Rakshaseshwara** (and the **anklet / nupura**).
  Not found in a Trincomalee-specific source; the Shiva of the headland is worshipped as Trikoneshwarar/Koneswarar.
- Sati body-part tradition: **conflicting.** Lists derived from the 18-peetha stotram commonly give **groin** at
  Trincomalee ("Trimkomali, Sankari devi"); one travel page says leg; the Pitha Nirnaya verse for "Lanka" gives
  **anklet**, with Indrakshi/Rakshaseshwara, and some sources place that Indrakshi peetha at **Nainativu** (Jaffna
  peninsula) rather than Trincomalee. The stotram itself names only the goddess and place, not the body part. The
  content therefore gives groin as the common list tradition, notes the anklet verse, and says traditions differ.
- Coordinates: **8.5825, 81.2456** (Koneswaram / Swami Rock, matching the existing `koneswarar-trincomalee` entry;
  Wikipedia lists the temple at 8°34'57"N 81°14'44"E). Shankari shrine's own point is not separately sourced; it is
  within the Koneswaram precinct, so this is a precinct-level coordinate.
- Defining legend: Sati/Daksha yajna, Shiva carrying her body, Vishnu's discus dividing it; each fallen part marks a
  peetha. Local tradition also links Swami Rock to Ravana (Koneswaram tradition).
- History: stotram ascribed to Adi Shankara (8th c.; used as `century` 8 because the founding is legend-only and
  the stotram is the earliest textual attestation; attribution itself is traditional). Portuguese governor
  Constantino de Sa de Noronha destroyed the Koneswaram complex including the Shankari shrine; **sources conflict on
  the year: 1622 (Wikipedia "Conquest of Koneswaram Temple", shubhpanchang) vs 1624 (behindeverytemple, Fort Frederick
  completed 1624)**. Stone reused for Fort Frederick. Shankari shrine reported rebuilt close to the original site in
  1952 (shubhpanchang; Koneswaram infobox gives restoration 1952); other sources give 1963 for Koneswaram
  inauguration. Content says only "twentieth century".
- Architecture: modern Dravidian reconstruction within the Koneswaram precinct; Swami Rock cliff ~120 m (400 ft) to
  the sea; approach through Fort Frederick gates. No surviving original stonework identified.
- Festivals: Navaratri/Vijayadashami and Maha Shivaratri are general Shakta/Shaiva observances; **no source found
  documenting a Shankari-shrine-specific festival calendar.**
- Access: Trincomalee town; Fort Frederick < 3 km from Trincomalee railway station; Colombo ~260 km by road
  (commonly cited, approximate); fort listed open 6:00-22:00 daily; foreign-visitor ticket counter in front of the
  Koneswaram car park (travel listings; fees not stated in content).

## Not verified
- The Sati body part at this peetha (groin vs anklet vs leg); the Bhairava specific to Shankari (Rakshaseshwara is from
  the Lanka verse); exact year of Portuguese destruction (1622 vs 1624); exact year/rebuilder of the modern Shankari
  shrine; the claims on shubhpanchang.com of Chola-era Kulakkottan renovation and hidden idols recovered c.1948 (not
  used in content); Shankari-specific festival dates; "Fridays dear to devotees" is a general Shakta practice, not a
  site-specific source; current temple timings and ticket rules (travel listings only); whether the Shankari shrine
  is a distinct building today or a shrine within the Koneswaram complex (sources say "next to Koneswaram").
