# Vishalakshi Temple, Varanasi (vishalakshi-varanasi)

Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Varanasyam Vishalakshi"). Mir Ghat, Varanasi (Kashi) district,
Uttar Pradesh, India. Tradition: Shaktism. Not a member of any circuit (circuits: []).
Distinct from the Shiva temple `kashi-vishwanath-varanasi`.

- Goddess: Vishalakshi (Vishalakshmi), "the wide-eyed"; also called Manikarni (Manikarnika
  Devi) in some accounts. Bhairava: Kala Bhairava (per Peetha Nirnaya Tantra-based listings).
- Sati body-part tradition: accounts differ. The commonly cited version is the gem of Sati's
  (right) earring (*manikarna*), which fell beside Manikarnika and gave the ghat its name;
  a second version says one of Sati's eyes (*akshi*) fell here, read as the origin of the name
  Vishalakshi. The Ashtadasha stotram (attributed to Adi Shankara) names the shrine only
  ("Varanasyam Vishalakshi"); it does not itself list body parts. The content text states
  both versions and attributes them to "tradition".
- Coordinates: **25.309, 83.011** (Wikipedia 25 deg 18'32"N 83 deg 0'39"E = 25.3089, 83.0108;
  AroundUs lists 25.30996, 83.01190 — ~100 m apart). Lane-level precision not verified.
- Defining legends: (1) Sati/Daksha yajna and the dispersal of her body; (2) Skanda Purana
  (Kashi Khanda) tale of Vyasa cursing Kashi when no household gave him food, and Vishalakshi
  appearing as a housewife to feed him. Kashi Khanda also counts her among the Nava Gauri
  (Vishalakshi Gauri); chapter references cited by a local temple guide (ch. 70 praise, ch. 100
  Nau Gauri yatra) — not independently checked.
- Architecture: Present temple completed 1893 (Wikipedia), rebuilt and maintained since the
  19th century by the Nagarathar community of Tamil Nadu. Haveli-like North Indian form with a
  modest Dravidian gopuram and Tamil inscriptions. Sanctum reportedly holds two dark-stone
  murtis: the older Adi Vishalakshi and a larger, jewelled one installed at the Tamil
  devotees' renovation. Dharmakupa stepwell nearby (Culture and Heritage of Varanasi).
- Festivals: Kajali Teej (Bhadrapada dark fortnight, Aug-Sep) given as principal festival by
  Wikipedia; Chaitra and Sharad Navratri; stop on the Nava Gauri yatra.
- Access: Mir Ghat, old city, near Manikarnika Ghat and Kashi Vishwanath. Varanasi Junction
  ~4.5 km (one source says 3 km); Lal Bahadur Shastri International Airport ~25 km (25-26 km
  sources). Lanes too narrow for vehicles near the ghats; final stretch on foot.
- **Not verified:** exact temple timings (sources conflict: 4:30-11:00 & 5:00-22:00; 5:30-21:00;
  6:00-22:00) — the file gives an approximate range and says to confirm; photography and dress
  rules at this specific shrine (general modest-dress advice only; no formal rule found); the
  equivalence Nagarathar = Nattukottai Chettiar (standard, but not stated in the sources
  consulted); the claim that Adi Shankara installed a Sri Yantra here (single promotional
  source, not used in the text); whether the earring is specifically the "right" one (Wikipedia
  says right; others unspecified, text says "ear-ornament"); the date and extent of any
  pre-1893 structure (the temple is described as ancient, but the pre-19th-century building
  history is not sourced); Kashi Vishwanath Corridor-era changes to lane access; exact Bhairava
  name sources beyond Peetha Nirnaya-based listings; century 19 is the date of the present
  structure, not of the pitha.
