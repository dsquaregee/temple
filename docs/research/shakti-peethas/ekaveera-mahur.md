# Renuka Devi (Ekaveerika) Temple, Mahur / Mahurgad (ekaveera-mahur)

Circuit: none yet (Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha; one of the three and a half Shakti Peethas of Maharashtra).
District/State: Nanded (Mahur taluka), Maharashtra. Tradition: Shaktism.

- Goddess + Bhairava: **Ekaveerika** in the Ashtadasha Shakti Peetha Stotram ("Mahurye Ekaveerika",
  attributed to Adi Shankaracharya); locally **Renuka Devi / Renuka Mata**, mother of Parashurama. The
  Bhairava at Mahur was **not found** in any source consulted -> not named in the content.
- Sati body-part tradition: **conflicting.** Wikipedia (Mahur, Maharashtra) says the **right arm** fell here
  (the same sentence appears on the Saptashrungi page, so attribution is shaky); hindupedia list gives
  "Ekavenika devi, Nanded"; another list says "Ekaveerika - Mahur, Nanded - Head"; a travel listing says
  "shoulder"; Maharashtra tourism / local legend: Renuka's **head** fell here after Parashurama beheaded her.
  Content states that traditions differ: right arm often named, local belief speaks of Renuka's head.
- Coordinates: **19.836, 77.926.** Based on Wikipedia "Mahur Fort" infobox 19°50'09.5"N 77°55'32.5"E
  (19.8360, 77.9257); Mahur town per Wikipedia 19°50'52"N 77°55'26"E (19.8478, 77.9239). The temple is on a
  hill ~2 km from Mahur village (Nanded district page). Exact temple-hilltop coordinate **not verified**
  (hill-level, +/- ~1 km).
- Defining legend: Renuka, wife of Jamadagni, mother of Parashurama; beheaded by her son on her husband's
  order; her head fell at Mahur (local tradition, tourism page, Wikipedia). The restoration to life by
  Parashurama's boon is the standard Puranic ending -- **not confirmed** in the search summaries; the
  Sahasrarjuna/Kamadhenu version on Wikipedia carries a "citation needed" tag and was omitted. Also
  Mahur = birthplace of Dattatreya (Datta Shikhar, highest hill, full idol rather than padukas); Anasuya
  temple on the third hill (Anasuya = wife of Atri, mother of Dattatreya).
- History/architecture: Temple attributed to the Yadavas of Devagiri -- 13th c. (one source), 12th c.
  (another), "eight or nine hundred years ago" (Nanded district site). Idol is swayambhu with only the
  head visible above ground (Outlook Traveller / tourism). Mahur Fort (Wikipedia): basalt, ruined, circuit six
  miles over two hills, Hathi Darwaja gate, Injala/Brahmatirth tank between hills; fort predates the Yadavas;
  ruled in turn by Gonds, Bahmani, Ahmadnagar, Mughals, Marathas, Nizam (infobox). Vedanta Bharati / temple
  authorities believe Adi Shankaracharya visited (tradition, not independently verified).
- Festivals: Sharadiya Navaratri with Ghatasthapana (AIR report, Oct 2026); big fair at Vijayadashami
  (Wikipedia, Nanded district site, utsav.gov.in "Renuka Mata Temple Mela").
- Access: Mahur ~125-130 km from Nanded (nearest main railway station; district site says airport 100 km,
  Kinwat railway 50 km); fort sites quote 130/140 km from Nanded. Frequent buses from Nanded bus stand.
  Temple ~2 km from the village; steps (a yatra guide says 200-250 steps; not used as number).
  Timings (third-party guide): ~6 AM-12 PM and 4-9 PM on Monday, ~6 AM-9 PM other days.
- **Not verified:** Bhairava of the peetha; Ekaveerika's identity with Renuka beyond the stotram's naming
  and local usage; Yadava date; temple architectural style/features beyond "stone hill temple"; exact number
  of steps; official timings and photography rules (written as "generally not allowed inside sanctum"
  by analogy with typical practice -- not confirmed for Mahur); Datta Jayanti timings; Renuka restoration
  episode detail; "far north-east of Nanded district" (general geography); precise coordinates. WebFetch
  was blocked; all facts are from search-result summaries.
