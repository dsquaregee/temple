# Sharada Peeth, Neelum Valley (sharada-peeth-kashmir)

Ashtadasha Maha Shakti Peetha ("Kashmireshu Saraswati"). Village of Sharda (Sharda tehsil),
Neelum District, Azad Jammu and Kashmir — Pakistan-administered Kashmir, across the Line of
Control from Indian-administered Kashmir (Kupwara district). Tradition: Shaktism.
circuits: [] (not a member of any circuit).

- Goddess: Sharada (Saraswati), goddess of learning; "Saraswati of Kashmir". Bhairava: one
  listing (Wikipedia-derived table) gives "Sharada Bhairava"; treated as unconfirmed and not
  used in the content text.
- Sati body-part tradition: the **right hand** fell here (Wikipedia, Anantam list, Organiser
  and others). The Ashtadasha stotram (attributed to Adi Shankara) names the seat only —
  "Kashmireshu Saraswati" — and does not itself list body parts.
- Coordinates: **34.792, 74.190** (Wikipedia: 34 deg 47'31"N 74 deg 11'24"E; Sharda tehsil
  page 34 deg 47'35"N 74 deg 11'19"E). Altitude ~1,981 m (6,499 ft) per Wikipedia. Within the
  Neelum Valley, on the Neelum (called Kishanganga on the Indian side). Point is village/site
  level. (A coordinate of 34.79, 73.7 floated in a query was checked and rejected; 74.19 is
  the sourced longitude.)
- Defining legends: (1) Sati's right hand fell in the valley; (2) the sage Shandilya's penance
  at Sharada Vana in the upper Kishenganga valley and his vision of the goddess Sharada
  (Tirtha Yatra / Kashmir history sources); (3) Adi Shankara said to have visited and received
  the goddess's blessing (greenmesg / Organiser; "tradition says").
- History: earliest textual reference cited as the Nilamata Purana (AJK Archaeology Dept);
  mentioned by Kalhana (Rajatarangini), incl. the episode of Gauda assassins arriving under
  the guise of a pilgrimage to Sharada during Lalitaditya's reign (713-755). Wikipedia: probably
  commissioned by Lalitaditya Muktapida (r. 724-760) though no definitive evidence. Centre of
  learning c. 6th-12th century; Sharada script associated (originated in Kashmir c. 7th-8th
  c.). Aurel Stein visited 1892 (Rajatarangini notes, Note B-137 "Shrine of Sharada"). One of
  the three holiest Kashmiri Pandit sites with Martand and Amarnath (Wikipedia). 2005
  earthquake damage; neglect; AJK/Friday Times conservation appeals.
- Architecture: stone temple on a raised plinth, approached by an imposing stone stair from
  the west; gateway with the usual Kashmiri double porch; square sanctum with a west-facing
  door; columns in a "Doric-like" description; surrounded by a quadrangle (Pakistan Heritage:
  142 x 94 ft). Pakistan Heritage's plinth figure ("24 square feet, 5.25 ft high") looks
  unit-garbled, and sources conflict on the roof (wooden planks vs. low shingle) — hence the
  content says dimensions and original roof details vary / are partly known.
- Festivals/pilgrimage: annual pilgrimage in the bright fortnight of Bhadrapada; sources
  differ on the day (4th shukla per the Mahatmya as cited; 8th/Ashtami in another). Content
  says "bright fortnight, accounts name different days". Not possible from India since 1947.
- Cross-LoC significance: Teetwal (Tithwal, Kupwara) crossing closed since August 2019. On 22
  March 2023 Amit Shah inaugurated a Sharada Devi temple at Teetwal, built by the Save Sharda
  Committee, and said the government would try to open a Sharada Peeth corridor on the
  Kartarpur model — not agreed with Pakistan as of the last source (Kashmir Life; The Week
  May 2025 reports the Pahalgam attack disrupted Teetwal pilgrimage). No evidence found that a
  corridor has opened.
- Access: Neelum Valley road (Muzaffarabad side) — general knowledge, route and permits **not
  verified**; Teetwal temple by road from Srinagar via Kupwara.
- **Not verified:** the distance Teetwal-Sharda Peeth (sources say "~40 km" and "across the
  river from Teetwal"; the content avoids a figure); the Bhairava name; the Bhadrapada pilgrimage
  day; whether Lalitaditya built it; the "university" scale and dates (6th-12th c. is repeated
  in secondary sources); Stein-era dimensions and roof; current condition after 2005 repairs;
  entry/permit conditions for visitors on the Pakistani side (including for non-Pakistani
  nationals); Teetwal temple timings; photography rules; the Madhumati/Sandili/Sargun stream
  names (sources vary; content only says "mountain streams meet"); the Shankara visit (tradition
  only); century 8 reflects the Lalitaditya-era attribution, not a proven date.
